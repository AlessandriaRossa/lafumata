The Tuesday evening online meeting began with the reporting of some news concerning the increasingly massive use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in various fields.

Microsoft's “medical” division has [developed](https://www.repubblica.it/tecnologia/2026/10/23/news/ia-microsoft-supera-medici-pronto-soccorso-science-425669419/) an AI system capable of carrying out a patient's diagnostic pathway based on the data contained in medical records: according to the developers, the results are remarkable and show greater accuracy than that of physicians. Microsoft's system employs a series of virtual agents that use different approaches: some analyse the data, others put forward hypotheses, others still request targeted examinations, highlighting specific aspects, proceeding cautiously and avoiding unnecessary tests. The CEO of Microsoft AI, Mustafa Suleyman, speaks openly of a “medical” superintelligence capable of monitoring the patient's health.

In an episode of “[Eta Beta](https://www.raiplaysound.it/audio/2026/10/Eta-Beta-del-24102026-4da2fb13-cab2-429f-88ab-7fd6b57204df.html)”, a program on Rai Radio 1, the topic of modern *makers* (“digital artisans”) was addressed – people who, also by using AI systems, develop projects and produce prototypes of every kind. With an AI system at one's disposal, it is no longer indispensable to be “specialised”, nor to hold an engineering degree, nor even to know how to program or use CAD: it is enough to formulate a request in natural language, and the system translates it into code or into a design.

A [complex system](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/01/complessita.htm) is emerging that manages to find solutions to a variety of problems. In mathematics, for example, new methods are being developed and conjectures that seemed inaccessible are being solved. AI succeeds at this because it can examine billions of pieces of information, something a human brain would struggle to do. The advancement of such systems has been made possible by the immense quantity of data that [our species](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/57/insiemi-modelli-previsione.htm) has produced and accumulated over time, which has now become raw material for machines.

The contemporary encyclopedia now resides on the Web, and Wikipedia is one of its manifestations. In the article “[L’intelligenza al tempo dei Big Data](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/56/l-intelligenza-al-tempo-dei-Big-Data.htm)”[^1] (*n*+1, no. 56), we saw how two “schools of thought” had formed: on one side, the defenders of the scientific method, and on the other, those who maintain that data would be capable of self-organising and that therefore theory would no longer be needed. Chris Anderson, who belongs to an ideological current springing from Silicon Valley's techno-libertarian environment, is among the supporters of the thesis according to which the flood of data would render the scientific method antiquated. In reality, the scientific method and statistical analysis—Big Data—interpenetrate: our knowledge proceeds by assemblages, thanks to sensors and systems that we have developed over the course of evolution, and intelligence emerges from the collective behaviour of cells, individuals, algorithms, etc.

In an article published in the *[Corriere della Sera](https://www.corriere.it/le-serie-del-corriere/scintille/perche-vincono-sempre-quelli-che-urlano.shtml)* and in an interview with *[Adnkronos](https://www.adnkronos.com/politica/cortei-la-fisica-dei-sistemi-complessi-nessuna-regia-a-milano-sono-soggetti-inediti-ha-ragione-mattarella_55114e4xZZlCXCvYIInCao)*, the physicist, writer, and science communicator Gabriella Greison proposes using the physics of complex systems to “understand” what is happening these days in the squares. The relationship between human beings and networks cannot be understood starting from philosophical or political categories; the manifestation of emergent collective behaviours can be read with the tools of physics (Giorgio Parisi also addresses these topics in the essay *Le simmetrie nascoste*[^2]). Attention is often directed to the individual who writes a communiqué, to the political formation of the participants in a demonstration, to their slogans or their flags, whereas what is fundamental is the social context that produces certain configurations, which in turn react back upon society, allowing a given phenomenon to feed itself. Evidently, if an organisational form “gains the upper hand”, it is because it connects in a new way something that already exists on the Web and manages to become an *[attractor](https://www.chicago86.org/lotte-in-corso/italia/generale/1480-l-autunno-di-lotta-della-gen-z)* for those who were looking for that kind of solution.

The “17 October Movement”, whose alleged organiser the police have reportedly identified, is not the product of a single mind but of a social process. The media write about the role of Russians, Iranians, and political Islam, who supposedly “created” the movement from behind a desk. To take to the streets and produce something new, all these actors are not needed: it is enough to look at bills and the shopping cart, which weigh on the vast majority of society.

The M1710 did nothing but register something that was “in the air” and summarise it in a few lines, producing effects on society: from the self-interested alarmism of newspapers and politicians to the word-of-mouth on *social media*, which synchronised the desire to take to the streets. What has happened in Italy in recent weeks fits into what is happening in the [rest of the world](https://it.euronews.com/2026/10/26/proteste-in-tutta-europa-da-francia-e-albania-a-italia-spagna-e-portogallo), from France to Portugal, from Albania to Serbia, from the USA to Brazil. The theft of the future is a slogan frequently heard in marches and read on signs, written and chanted above all by the younger generations. It is a fine confirmation of what we wrote in the article “[Dov’è finito il Futuro?](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/46/che-fine-ha-fatto-il-futuro.htm)”[^3] (*n*+1, no. 46).

*Limes* devotes a video to the same theme (“[La fine del futuro. Le proteste in Francia e Italia. Malessere dei giovani e frattura generazionale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dEKNosovv4)”[^4]), and its editors themselves reach the conclusion that the young people taking to the streets have no prospects under current conditions, between precarity, misery, wars, crises, etc. The “thug” smashing shop windows in the centre of Milan is a product of this society, and forces it to take sides, to [polarise](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/banlieue_mondo.htm).

In recent weeks, tens of thousands of protesters have taken to the streets in all the major Italian cities. The generational aspect matters for the organisational methods because the protagonists are above all young and very young people, who have a different relationship with technology and *social networks*. During the detention of the young people, especially in Milan, the police confiscated precisely their *smartphones*, searching for networks of relationships. From the photos and videos of the demonstrations of 17 October and especially 24 October, it is clear that the floods of people in the various squares cannot be framed with the classic right/left schema: the social turmoil involves everyone – young people who had never taken to the streets before, occupied high schools and universities, workers and truck drivers – forcing the trade unions to call general strikes.

The “Third-Internationalist” conceptions of class struggle are, all things considered, forms of gradualist idealism: the factory worker is mythologised – the one in blue overalls – and that part of the proletariat that has neither a contract nor a flesh-and-blood boss, but works occasionally, perhaps through an *app*, is ignored. Millions of young precarious workers, with no horizon other than traineeships, internships, and fixed-term contracts, do not exist for these workerists past their time limit. Those who were once considered atypical workers have become typical, and vice versa. The 1997 UPS strike had already shown how super-precarious drivers, using their everyday work tools, were capable of striking and gathering great solidarity for their struggle (“[I sedici giorni più belli](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/03/sedici_giorni.htm)”[^5], *n*+1, no. 3).

The future already acts upon the present and selects its own instruments, determining extinctions and evolutions. The true “unprecedented subject” is the social brain of the revolution, an organism that is clearing out the politicking approach to communism. There is an inexorable tendency toward the dissolution of parties, little groups, and tiny parties, while an *anti-formist* movement is making its way – one whose existence the “Third-Internationalists” do not even suspect (“[Layout of Our Work Programme](https://www.quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/layout_work_programme.htm)”, “[Forward, Barbarians!](https://www.quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/threadoftime/082_1951_forward_barbarians.htm)”).

In Italy alone, according to [bourgeois estimates](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/La-poverta-in-Italia-2025.pdf), over 5.4 million people live in absolute poverty. Capitalist society is a structure that accumulates contradictions over the years, until at a certain point discontinuities occur. Truck drivers block the roads, students occupy schools, trade unions organise general strikes. But a photograph of the event is not enough: *cinematography* is needed – a metaphor indicating that every event must be traced back to the overall dynamic.
