The Tuesday evening online meeting began with an analysis of the mobilisations underway in France and Spain. For years we have been recording that the “social temperature” is rising, to the point of triggering catastrophic-type phenomena such as massive demonstrations, revolts, and riots. In a 2019 [newsletter](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/newsletter/nws_237.htm) we wrote that what is underway is a single great revolt against the law of value. In both France, where students are the protagonists of the mobilisations, and Spain, where the demonstrations and the Acampada in Puerta del Sol began after the eviction of an elderly woman, the trade unions were also involved.

In Spain, the UGT and the Comisiones Obreras (CCOO) have called a 24-hour general strike to demand a wage increase that would make it possible to cope with housing costs. According to union sources consulted by *El País*, the date has not yet been set, but the strike will take place before the political elections of 29 November, announced on Monday by prime minister Pedro Sánchez. In France, in addition to the student protests, on 29 September there was a national strike of public employees, with about 170 demonstrations called by the unions against the loss of purchasing power of wages. In both cases, the difficulty of surviving on current wages and the growth of inflation and rents have driven “social molecules” into the streets.

In the article “[Marasma sociale e guerra](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/29/marasma_sociale_e_guerra.htm)”[^1], we outlined a process that over the years has manifested itself increasingly clearly: *“What we are analysing is a seismic wave whose underground energy is the same for all the different surface phenomena; when here a wall collapses, there a chasm opens, and elsewhere a landslide falls”.*

In the article “[Wargame. Parte seconda](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/51/wargame-parte-seconda.htm)”[^2], a hypothetical scenario is described, which begins with a demonstration and continues with the occupation of a square; the protesters' ideas are confused, but gradually mechanisms of action and reaction are set in motion that lead the organism formed in the square to become something other than what the participants themselves had imagined.

In nature, self-organisation processes exist at every level: after long periods of apparent stagnation, sudden changes occur, as in the “punctuated equilibrium theory” of Niles Eldredge and Stephen Jay Gould. Human society is crossed by opposing interests due to the existence of classes; this produces conflicts of varying nature and intensity. Today, in the occupied squares, there is an interclassist muddle: proletarians are there too, but a general direction is lacking, and the influence of the dominant ideology has not disappeared. Nevertheless, the great disorientation may be the premise for a generalised change of direction. The party of the dominant class manages to stay standing because it has historically been supported by the middle classes. For some time now, it is precisely the middle classes that are becoming agitated, worried about plunging into the circle of the reserveless. The bourgeoisie thus clashes, before it clashes with the proletarian class, with components internal to its own party (understood in the broad sense).

This is an irreversible historical process: there can be no return to the great mass parties or the great trade union centres; the revolution is on the march when it dissolves the old trappings of politics. If an *anti-formist* movement were to arise, it could not fall behind what Occupy Wall Street achieved, all the more so since, compared with 2011–2012, the development of the productive forces has made giant strides with automated systems, artificial intelligence, and robotics. The bifurcation does not depend on someone's will, but on the preceding historical conditions, as well as on the movement's capacity to criticise itself. The only real alternative is to turn one's back on the past and turn toward the programme that comes from the future. While it is true that the collapse of Stalinism is visible in the squares, it is also true that criticism of Third-Internationalism, which reappears under false guises, must not be relaxed.

In several works of *n*+1 we have demonstrated how the movement, not its cause, is invariant. The anger of young students in France and of tenants tired of having their lives stolen to pay rent is the effect of the dissolution of the current mode of production. On one side there is the crumbling of the State, and on the other the emergence of an organism that seeks, by trial and error, to free itself from the homogenising leaden blanket.

Our work is a research on the attempts at future organisation present within capitalism. During the last work meeting (3–4 October), we highlighted how the “political” approach to the theme of social transitions is outdated and harmful. Occupy Wall Street had managed to emancipate itself from the political soul of revolution thanks to a way of proceeding completely different from what had been seen in recent decades. Not only had it turned its back on “politics”, but it had sought to lay the foundations of the new environment: although it intervened with its organisational network in struggles—from the blockade of West Coast ports to flying pickets—OWS had as its main objective the new society and fought for it. From the images and videos that circulated online, one perceives the enthusiastic, joyful, and vital atmosphere that emerged from the occupied squares.

Strong social tensions are bringing back to light texts, memories, and recollections that seemed forgotten: in Spain, since 26 September, the date of the encampment at Puerta del Sol, a [peak](https://diariosocialista.net/2026/10/05/las-busquedas-de-el-manifiesto-comunista-se-disparan-mas-de-un-5-000-en-el-estado-espanol/) in Google searches for the *Manifesto of the Communist Party* has reportedly been recorded. The movement that started in Madrid immediately spread throughout the country. An issue concerning excessively high rents has turned into a much bigger problem for the State. The Acampada has brought about a political-institutional crisis, with the early end of the legislature and the proclamation of a general strike; one glimpses the attempt to unify all aspects (wages, housing, work). The struggle may also recede, but only to reappear in the future more radical than before.

During the work meeting, it was reaffirmed that politics is a superstructure almost entirely irrelevant to the becoming of society. This way of interpreting historical dynamics is the one inherited from the “Italian” Communist Left (“[La dottrina dei modi di produzione](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/ennepiuuno/dottrinamodi.htm)”[^3]). The end of the revolution with a political soul means the emergence of revolution on a human basis. The technocratic movement of the 1930s, today's Venus Project, as well as OWS, rejected the political approach: the technocrats wanted to overcome accounting in terms of value, the Venus Project aims to create communities without money or wage labour, and OWS sought to give life to a human community. These are all experiments that communists study, elaborate, and synthesise, raising them to a higher level (“[Marcati sintomi di società futura](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/34/marcati_sintomi_societa_futura.htm)”[^4]).

During all revolutions, an *[anti-form](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/58/le-determinazioni-materiali-e-l-ambiente.htm)* environment has imposed itself, one that has been able to anticipate certain aspects of the future society. Our small environment, from websites to *social media*, can exert an influence online to the extent that it defends the continuity of a method of work and of a specific theory. Revolution is a physical phenomenon, and as with other physical phenomena, what matters is to establish the critical point at which a profound modification of the collective properties of the system manifests itself.

On 3 and 4 October we also addressed the theme of overcoming the dichotomy between the physical and social sciences, reaffirming the need for a unified science – a problem that Marxism had already placed on theoretical ground with the *[Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1844/manuscripts/preface.htm)*. Phase transitions must be analysed with scientific rather than political criteria, through data, models, and schemas, and not through sterile debates. The physicist Giorgio Parisi wrote an essay to show that physics lies at the basis of AI development: metastable systems, interactions that produce collective behaviours, second-order phase transitions. When one describes a ferromagnetic material undergoing a phase transition and exhibiting global magnetisation, one is speaking more or less directly of what is happening in the French and Spanish squares.

The Left's schema of the reversal of praxis (“[Theory and Action in Marxist Doctrine](https://intcp.org/en/texts/10124/theory-and-action-in-marxist-doctrine/)”, 1951) depicts a *feedback* loop between class, intermediate organisations, and party – a flow of information and energy back and forth, a universal model of self-organisation that is useful to us for understanding what happens in society, especially when social polarisation occurs. In complex systems, the effect returns to the structure that generated it, modifying its subsequent behaviour. The current to which we refer has an engineering conception of revolution, a conception on which we pivot and which distinguishes us from the *milieu* that lays claim to communism.

For months we have been collecting material on the financial bubble of artificial intelligence. In the 2008 crisis, the sector “targeted” by speculation was real estate; today it is AI's turn. From the trajectory of surplus value to the trend of the rate of profit, diagrams are essential for describing the trajectory of capitalist society and establishing the timing of its catastrophe. From the squares rising up all over the world to the war waged by machine systems, from the collapse of States to financial bubbles, from world debt to “natural” disasters, the planet is converging toward a [singularity](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/40/verso_la_singolarita_storica.htm).
