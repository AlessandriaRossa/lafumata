The Tuesday evening online meeting began with some considerations on the recent demonstrations in Italy and France. The communiqué of the “17 October Movement” was noted, significant because it is the product of what is happening in the Italian squares. Published *online* on Tuesday, 20 October, within a few hours it was picked up by the major newspapers. The movement's [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/movimento17ottobre/) page is followed by thousands of subscribers, who also coordinate via Telegram. The M1710 takes its name precisely from 17 October, when there were clashes in Milan, Turin, Bologna, and Brescia, and again the following day in Milan.

The communiqué takes up several elements: the need, on the one hand, for the collective growth of the squares, and on the other, for approaches different from the past, such as the fact that the movement does not define itself as an acronym, but as a process, and above all declares itself autonomous from parties, trade unions, but also from movementism.

This is an “operational” communiqué based on a few points: we do not trust the institutions and this government, we do not trust the classic political structures, we protect ourselves from the repression of the State and of the fascists, we do not want a seat at the table of those who have taken our chair away: we want to build another table, all together, from below. In the short section titled “Fluidity Is a Strategy, and We Make It Our Own”, the struggles of recent years are recalled, from those in Hong Kong to Occupy Wall Street, up to the mobilisations of Generation Z in Morocco. The communiqué calls for taking to the streets every Saturday starting from 24 October.

The M1710, regardless of who is [behind](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/cronaca/2026/10/18/la-giornata-di-scontri-in-piazza-dalla-chiamata-sui-social-da-una-sigla-sconosciuta_59d80aae-d045-4d8d-98e9-8e43612a389b.html) it, is a cybernetic device that *draws* information from the network, *processes* it, and *reinserts* it in modified form. Society produces demonstrations, blockades, and clashes; a part of it processes what is happening and writes a communiqué that becomes a “meme” – a piece of information that begins to live a life of its own and evolves.

Law enforcement is on alert because they found themselves unprepared in the face of the latest demonstrations, especially in Milan. The media indeed speak of the [young age](https://milano.corriere.it/notizie/cronaca/26_ottobre_17/milano-chi-sono-i-manifestanti-degli-scontri-in-piazza-duomo-giovanissimi-non-politicizzati-violenti-come-in-francia-181923d9-8a96-47bd-aab5-12a53fa38xlk.shtml) of most of those detained and their lack of political experience, of the fact that they are not coordinated by a centre but act in a fluid manner, without a leader or references to any organisation. This is the era of revolts conducted by *swarm intelligence*.

A *wargame* is underway between protesters and security apparatuses, and both are forced to refine strategies and tactics. This game tends to involve the whole of society, which is becoming increasingly polarised. As we wrote in “[Banlieue è il mondo](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/banlieue_mondo.htm)”[^1] (*n*+1, no. 19):

> *“Now someone can (perhaps) understand why a suburban thug, besides being the degenerate material product of a putrefied capitalism, is at the same time an equally material element, a factor of rupture, of alignment, of polarisation. It is happening, because the thug has forced everyone to take sides, expanding the banlieue to the entire world.”*

In moments of crisis and social turmoil, memories that seemed erased from history resurface, along with texts that few or no one was reading and forgotten social practices. Intelligence is the ability to foresee the future and possible scenarios, and therefore to intervene. The programme is precisely an operational plan oriented toward the future.

Our *mission* (the website of the [*n*+1 Foundation](https://www.fondazione-ennepiuuno.org/) is *online*) is to keep alive the memory of the “Italian” Communist Left, a movement of explorers in tomorrow, and to continue its work *on the thread of time*. It all holds together: from the rediscovery by millions of young people of Karl Marx's works, to the revolts against the cost of rents and the rising cost of living, all the way to the struggles against war and environmental devastation.

In the big cities, immediate territorial organisms will tend to emerge, capable of unifying the demands of a diffused proletariat – more precarious than ever, no longer with the old points of reference, but connected online. The occupied squares of Madrid, Lisbon, and Athens become material places for gathering protesters, experiences, and knowledge.

Social networks make it possible to overcome geographical distances and *bypass* the classic structures of social mediation. This process also runs through the trade unions, which are forced to ride the wave. The CGIL[^2] has [announced](https://www.cgil.it/strumenti/scioperi-e-vertenze/cgil-proclamazione-sciopero-generale-per-il-giorno-20-novembre-2026-x0ylgq91) a general strike for 20 November; USB and other rank-and-file unions have called one for [30 October](https://www.usb.it/leggi-notizia/sciopero-generale-30-ottobre-2026-2304.html). Similar dynamics have been seen in France and Spain. Trade union organisations involve millions of wage earners, so the union bosses, when they perceive the rise in social temperature, “[activate](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/10/18/cgil-indetto-sciopero-generale-20-novembre/)” themselves. Trade unions, however much they are led by “traitorous” leaders, are for the most part composed of wage earners. What can be said of parliament – an organ of the bourgeois State in which it is claimed that all classes of society are represented – cannot therefore be said of the trade unions, economic professional associations that gather elements of one and the same class. In “[Revolutionary Party and Economic Action](https://intcp.org/en/texts/10124/theory-and-action-in-marxist-doctrine/#II_%E2%80%93_Revolutionary_Party_and_Economic_Action)” (1951), we find written:

> *“[...] any prospect of a general revolutionary movement will depend on the presence of the following essential factors: 1) a large, numerous proletariat of pure wage-earners, 2) a sizeable movement of associations with an economic content including a large part of the proletariat, 3) a strong revolutionary class party, which, composed of a militant minority of workers, must have been enabled in the course of the struggle to oppose, broadly and effectively, its own influence within the union movement to that of the bourgeois class and bourgeois power.”*

It can happen that trade unions are dragged into the struggle and lose control of their own base, or that trade union organisations are absorbed into processes larger than themselves, as in the case of the 2011 West Coast port blockade called by Occupy. In “Revolutionary Party and Economic Action” it is stated that *“the general line of the perspective outlined here does not rule out that there will be all kinds of different situations arising in the course of the modification, dissolution, and reconstitution of associations of the union type”.*

In 2025, driven by the protests for Palestine, the CGIL was forced to call a general strike with only a few days' notice. Union members are reckoning with inflation, the loss of the purchasing power of wages, and the general worsening of living conditions; the leaderships are forced to act when society becomes agitated. In the squares these days there are present, above all, young people who do not work and probably never will. The trade unions, in a crisis of consensus, precisely in order not to remain cut off, must try to connect with youth hardship and to represent it (“[Quelle dovrebbero essere anche nostre piazze](https://radicidelsindacato.org/2026/10/19/quelle-dovrebbero-essere-anche-nostre-piazze/)”[^3]).

[Albania](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/20/horizontal-revolt-why-albanias-young-protesters-want-pm-rama-to-go), Belgium, France, Italy, Spain, India, [Portugal](https://www.democrata.es/it/internazionale/arrestate-55-persone-nello-sgombero-del-campeggio-per-la-casa-a-lisbona/), Tunisia: the list of countries touched by “social turmoil” is long. The current synchronisation of the squares recalls the period when we wrote “[Mille città, l'ondata internazionale di manifestazioni del 15 ottobre 2011](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/volantini/mille_citta.htm)”[^4], even though we have not yet reached the level attained by Occupy (1/99%).

In France, students began demonstrating at the end of September, then public employees and the Gilets Jaunes joined in. The social clash that erupted on 17 October in Paris and other French cities could be repeated on Saturday, 24 October. Businesses, institutional buildings, and police stations were targeted; in Limoges the town hall was set on fire. In some Paris neighborhoods a [curfew](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/10/20/coprifuoco-francia-rinnovati-parigi-lione-bordeaux-marsiglia-nizza-tolosa/?bitPosition=2) is in force, some central metro stations are closed, and helicopters and drones monitor from above: the State is trying to shield itself as best it can.

The Italian government has sent another 500 soldiers to Milan and has issued a decree introducing preventive detention for minors and the crime of violent participation in marches, punishable by up to five years in prison, and providing for fines and compensation charged to parents for damage caused by their minor children. When there is a risk that protests will become radicalised, the State resorts to heavy-handed measures. In a situation of growing conflict, however, repression risks fanning the flames instead of extinguishing them. The demonstrations in the major cities go hand in hand with the blocking of supplies of goods and energy, caused by the protests of farmers and truck drivers in [Southern Italy](https://napoli.repubblica.it/cronaca/2026/10/20/news/tir_protesta_autostrada_traffico_blocchi-425659695/?ref=RHLF-BG-P2-S2-T1-r3837). The result is empty supermarkets and dry gas stations.

In recent days, the strike of the workers of the former Ilva[^5] plants in Taranto and [Genoa](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/10/19/i-lavoratori-ex-ilva-di-cornigliano-occupano-laeroporto-colombo-di-genova/8542773/) took place; in the metalworking sector there are numerous open disputes, involving tens of thousands of workers.

Evidently a critical threshold is being reached, beyond which the old equilibrium breaks. The unrest mainly concerns the new generations, who perceive not only the difficulties of living in the present, but above all the fact that [their future has been stolen](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/46/che-fine-ha-fatto-il-futuro.htm) from them.

The president of the Republic, Sergio Mattarella, speaking at the national assembly of the Italian provinces, stated that *“an intelligent and in-depth analysis is needed”*, *“a careful vigilance capable of countering violence”* coming from *“unprecedented actors”*, a condition that raises *“questions to be reflected upon from various angles”.*

The conservation party's *[wargame](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/51/wargame-parte-seconda.htm)* is being rapidly updated.
