The online meeting on Tuesday evening started with a comment on the essay by [Nello Cristianini](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxsV2lFLVUw), *Forma mentis. La corsa per decifrare i pensieri delle macchine*[^1], and on the article by Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, "[The Urgency of Interpretability](https://darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability)". Both texts focus on the information processing that occurs in the artificial neural networks at the foundation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems.

According to Amodei, mankind’s race to stay in step with the machines is wound up with the fact that no one knows exactly what happens within them. The lack of comprehension of the mechanisms that guide their functioning impedes the prediction of developments in the short term and raises important questions of [security](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/mondo/2026/06/13/anthropic-sospende-i-modelli-ia-piu-potenti-su-pressioni-governo-usa_77cb9e8a-fb0e-4bd2-b8aa-9b3da590c092.html):

> *"\[. . .\] generative AI systems are grown more than they are built—their internal mechanisms are emergent rather than directly designed \[. . .\] People outside the field are often surprised and alarmed to learn that we do not understand how our own AI creations work."*

Per Cristianini, [exploring](https://www.wired.it/article/intervista-nello-cristianini-big-interview/) the "black box" in AI systems requires three levels of abstraction. The "micro" level – represented by single elements, that is, by single neurons and their functioning – is not sufficient to comprehend the processes that lead to elaborate outputs.

At the second level, which Cristianini calls "meso"[^2], one can observe the formation of cognitive maps and neural networks, that is, the ensemble of the billions of connections between the nodes of the system (partially analogous to those of the human brain). The analysis of this level would make it possible to intervene on the network’s architecture, modifying some approaches and, consequently, the results produced by AI. We read in *Forma mentis*: *The key isn’t in individual neurons, but in the architecture of the network that connects them"*.

The "macro" level, which covers the continuous interactions between machines and men and machines and machines, is where one can study the outputs AI produces relative to its *inputs*. Ascribing to an animal or to a machine the capability of "intentional behaviour" doesn’t mean ascribing consciousness to them. Even single-cell organisms move towards a food source in a deterministic way: can one say they think? The machine also exhibits a behaviour and is the result of an evolutionary history as a complex system, describable only through the interaction between its parts and between the system and the external environment, men included.

According to some philosophers, large language models (LLMs) do nothing but statistically combine data, akin to "stochastic parrots" (Emily M. Bender, Timnit Gebru, and Angelina McMillan-Major). In reality, AI systems can build dynamic maps (internal representations of the problem’s status), for example a chessboard (AlphaZero), allowing forms of learning and planning. The parrot doesn’t play chess, the machine does and beats the best chess players.

Anthropic is investigating precisely this "psychological" level of the machine, with the goal of modifying behaviors deemed [dangerous](https://www.corriere.it/tecnologia/26_giugno_15/furti-incendi-aggressioni-il-reality-show-di-dieci-agenti-ai-lasciati-liberi-in-una-citta-virtuale-commettono-crimini-f0524d96-8d1a-48dd-9a54-8b2a0db36xlk.shtml?utm_source=chatgpt.com) (the so-called AI alignment problem). To intervene in that regard, actions are taken on the environment, on the context, and on the  *inputs* provided. However, the different results that AI comes to don’t tell us how it came to a specific elaboration. According to the multidisciplinary approach, the comprehension of such processes can be improved by combining physics, maths, philosophy and psychology. Interdisciplinarity tends to reproduce the division of knowledge – a reflection of the social division of labour – by attempting to reassemble it without, however, overcoming it. On the contrary, a monistic approach to knowledge (organic, we’d say) is fundamental: world unitarity founded on unequivocal laws of matter-energy and thus for the living human-society (*n*+1, n. 15-16).

The works of Nello Cristianini and Dario Amodei answer to a pressing need of interpretability of machines and their potentiality, in light of the exponential progress of AI systems. We find ourselves in a borderland between the present and the future. "More is different" is the title of a notable article of the physicist and Nobel prize winner Philip Warren Anderson – in which the concept of "emergence" is introduced – understood as the appearance of new characteristics or behaviors that derive from the interaction of many simple components, without appearing in each isolated component.

When an interaction between elements occurs (as in the case of the primordial soup) new properties emerge. Such characteristics lack meaning if applied to a single atom. In the AI field, this new "mind" that has been taking shape wasn’t designed by someone in particular, but emerged from the immense social labour of research centers, start ups, academies, and industries (chip, circuits, server, data center, satellites, etc.).

Artificial neural networks simulate biological networks. In the LLM, when references to determined concepts are touched, similar internal configurations to the activations identified in the neuroscientific field in the study of the human brain are observed. AI is an enhancement of human intelligence and part of the social brain, which is both biological and extra biological. In the attempt to try to interpret what happens inside the "black box", we begin to understand that the real mystery to be solved is how humans themselves understand, that is, what are the processes that make consciousness possible.

According to Engels, the motor of our evolution is the [dynamic relation](https://www.marxists.org/italiano/marx-engels/1876/6/pr-umani.htm) between labour, hand, and brain. Mankind has evolved [working socially](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/38/fare_dire_pensare_sapere.htm) and, in this process, has transformed matter, social relations and even its own neurons. The human species had to equip itself with more and more complex tools to not succumb, "understanding" the world through tools, models, machines to know, and etcetera.

The bourgeoisie is addressing the issue of control because it recognises the emergence of something powerful: a form of intelligence that doesn’t belong to a single individual, but to the accumulated knowledge of the entire society. Marx, who tackled analogous questions in the first Volume of *Capital* and in the "Fragment on Machines" of the *Grundrisse*, analyses not only how machines work but also the [social relations](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/necessarie_dissoluzioni.htm) that they produce and those that they plunge into crisis. If the artisan used to own his means of labour and had specific skills, in the modern factory the worker’s knowledge is subsumed by the machine system, which has evolved so far as to give itself a brain complete with artificial neural networks, distributed not only inside the factory but on a planetary scale. Wealth "adjusted" to labour time no longer makes any sense, since the immediate productive force is taken over by the *General Intellect*:

> *"The human species, whose Life is History, has its own Brain, an organ built from its millennia-old function, which isn’t the legacy of any Skull or Cranium. The Knowledge of the species and Science, more than Gold, aren’t for us private heritages, and in Potentiality belong intact to the social Man."* ("[Traiettoria e catastrofe della forma capitalistica…](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/1952_1970/traiettoria_catastrofe1.htm)"[^3])

Information isn’t stored in some particular neuron, but it is distributed throughout the brain. Parallel connections allow us to develop models and schemas. The machine built itself "a picture" of what a bridge, an essay, or an email is, and can work with abstract concepts: 

> *"The goal of natural minds isn’t to form perfect and infallible knowledge, but descriptions of its own environment that are useful in reaching its own purposes. Machines too can form such internal representations and use them to guide their own behaviors. Between omniscience and perfect ignorance there’s a space, in which we all live"* (*Forma mentis*)

Machines aren’t just tools and – once a certain level of complexity and automation of functions is reached – they prove to be difficult to control for a society that cares first and foremost for profit.
In a YouTube [interview](https://cosasposta.substack.com/p/anthropic-non-si-capisce-dallitalia), Gian Segato – a researcher at Anthropic – observes how there is difficulty in understanding the exponential tendency of AI models and the fact that the sector’s innovation rate is extremely rapid. These models’ evolution will usher in the substitution of plenty of tasks, the [disappearance](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/42/lavorare_e_bello.htm) of many professions and millions of jobs, and, thus, causing a profound transformation in society. Cristianini recommends thinking of AI as an alien mind: this may be, but it must be specified that this is only because we live in an alienated social form. AI is only the latest manifestation of the development of industry, and from the Marx of the *1844 Manuscripts* we know that [true anthropology](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/genesi_uomo_industria.htm) is industry. We are nature that thinks, which is like saying that nature produced man-industry in order to think about itself. This cybernetic humanity (a symbiosis of natural and artificial), once freed from capitalism, will land on a superior social form, *n*+1.
