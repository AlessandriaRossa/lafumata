The online meeting last Tuesday evening started with commentary on two texts: "[Necessarie dissoluzioni](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/necessarie_dissoluzioni.htm)"[^1] (*n*+1, n. 36) and "[Ricapitolazione del lavoro di partito sulla legge marxista della caduta tendenziale del saggio di profitto e sulla analoga tendenza nell’incremento relativo della produzione industriale](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/programma-comunista/1967/13/19670727_ricapitolazione-caduta-tendenziale.htm)"[^2] (*Il programma comunista*, 1967).

Those texts are fundamental for framing phenomena such as Artificial Intelligence and their social repercussions. In "Ricapitolazione del lavoro di partito…"[^2] a few quotes from *Capital* (Volume III, Third edition) are listed, useful for a further development of our studies. Marx demonstrates how the rate of profit *"falls in a historical and tendential manner (that is, not in a simple and linear way, but through an irregular course) proportionally to the growing organic composition of capital; that is, to the relation between the constant and variable parts inside the total capital paid in advance by the capitalist."*

The rate of profit is obtained by dividing the profit (P) by the constant capital (C) added to the variable capital (V): if the share of variable capital decreases, this means that the only source of surplus value reduces, which then gets divided between the parasitic classes under the form of profit/interest and revenue:

> *"This is only to say that the same number of workers, or the same quantity of labour-power, made available by variable capital of a given value-magnitude,—as a consequence of the distinct methods of production developing within capitalist production—create an ever-growing mass of the means of labour, machinery, and all sorts of fixed capital, as well as raw and auxiliary materials, which at the same time set into motion more processes and productive consumption; hence, an ever-growing value-magnitude of constant capital."*

The development of machine systems (today having become intelligent) eliminates, potentially, enormous numbers of wage labourers, transforming them into unproductive surplus population, useless for capitalism as they are unable even to consume.

> *"[...] since the aggregate mass of living labour operating the means of production decreases in relation to the value of these means of production, it follows that the unpaid labour and the portion of value in which it is expressed must decline as compared to the value of the advanced total capital. Or: an ever smaller aliquot part of invested total capital is converted into living labour, and this total capital, therefore, absorbs in proportion to its magnitude less and less surplus-labour, although the unpaid part of the labour applied may at the same time grow in relation to the paid part."*. 

Capital necessarily has to increase the quantity of unpaid labour, that is, the rate of surplus value (P/V), but to achieve that it increases the quantity of constant capital (machines, technique, science). Historically, dead labour increases enormously compared to living labour, causing the whole system of wage labour to detonate.

> *"The progressive tendency of the general rate of profit to fall is, therefore, just *an expression peculiar to the capitalist mode of production* of the progressive development of the social productivity of labour."*

Marx proves how the increase in labour productivity is the [historic doom](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/filitempo/061_1951_nonpotete.htm) of capitalism. Capital works for its own dissolution, laying the foundations for a higher social form. The bourgeoisie will never recognise the validity of the law of the falling rate of profit (while nobody questions *auxology*, the discipline that studies the decreasing increments in biology), since it would be equivalent to admitting that the capitalist mode of production is destined to disappear.

To make up for the overall fall in the rate of profit, capitalism is forced to magnify the scale of commodity production; this leads to the necessity of conquering, by means fair or foul, of new markets (Lenin, *Imperialism*). Industrial production, theoretically infinite, encounters instead the truly finite limits of the Planet (J. Rifkin, *Entropy*). The enormity of the current capitalistic dissipation[^3]  has led the world to consume more resources than can be regenerated annually by as early as June (“Overshoot-day” 2026), thus the solution to the problem is not to find a treatment for the ill, but to eliminate the illness.

The *antagonistic reasons* (increase in the degree of labour exploitation, reduction of wage below its value, decrease in the price of the elements of constant capital, relative overpopulation, foreign trade, increase of finance capital) are unable to [counteract](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/40/pensiamoci_bene.htm) the fall in the absolute rate of profit, they can only shift the contradictions further down the line. This system has exhausted its historic function: any *reformist* hypothesis has already been tried, all that remains is to travel the *anti-formist* road, the one described in "[Layout of Our Work Programme](https://quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/layout_work_programme.htm)" (1946).

Marx says that it’s the fall of the rate of profit, thus the jam of the mechanisms of capital valorisation, that determines crises, financial speculations and an excess of capital, together with an excess of population. The capitalist mode of production is transitory, since its own development produces the conditions of its dissolution. Inside this great dissolution that we are living in, micro-dissolutions can be identified at the historical scale of the last century, such as third-internationalist language, the *battilocchio*, printed media, the religion of labour, syncretic programmes, democratic centralism, the party apparatus, etc.

AI was trained with data, texts, and information previously selected. A “neural map” of the world is being structured in a way that allows for further bio-cybernetic evolution. In nature, there exist [organisms](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/44/dai-batteri-a-bach-e-ritorno.htm) and complex systems that implement adaptive behaviours (such as hunting, the construction of nests or the design of a bridge), even without any cognition of biology, physics or engineering.  *Large Language Models* (LLMs) generate texts and solve problems by using statistical patterns, demonstrating a high practical competence in producing adequate answers. Human and artificial neurons have been “trained” through interaction with the environment, the former through an evolution that lasted for hundreds of thousands of years, the latter in only a couple of years. However, our unified theory of knowledge tells us that it is wrong to consider them in opposition, because AI is an aspect of human intelligence, an extension of it, and is an active component of the *General Intellect* (Marx, *Grundrisse*). Alienation is the condition in which the worker is separated from the products of their labour, from the productive process and from their social relations. Once the [social division of labour](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/12/abolizione_mestieri.htm) and the commodity form have been overcome, alienation itself will be overcomed.

Automated production, financial bubbles, a continuously expanding army of those without reserves: the parameters of capitalism are completely off. In China, 20 million proletarians have [registered](https://www.china-files.com/in-cinque-per-un-solo-ordine-la-cina-ha-troppi-rider/) for *app* platforms that facilitate the home delivery of goods, despite a real demand of only around 4 million workers. Within the whole country in 2025, the *gig economy* sector has employed almost 300 million people. But the tendency towards high-tech slavery – without rights, contracts, or even a boss to blame – is a global phenomenon.

The historic limit of capitalism is shown not only by the course of the rate of profit, but also by energetic and ecological tendencies (“[Un modello dinamico di crisi](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/24/modello_dinamico_01.htm)”)[^4]. When one speaks of AI there often is a tendency to imagine it as something impalpable, as if it is only composed of algorithms. In reality, AI depends on material infrastructure, such as *datacentres*, that require enormous quantities of water for cooling and electrical energy for working. Recently, an opposition movement to the creation of new *datacentres* has emerged in the USA, while in China some are being built underwater, powered by onshore wind farms and cooled by exploiting ocean currents.

AI systems, trained on large quantities of data, could reach  "rational" conclusions on the production/distribution of goods and suggest to society projects for reducing dissipation[^3]. Considerations of this kind were already formulated by some components of the bourgeoisie in the ‘70s, by exposing the limits of growth (MIT, Club of Rome). The only anti-entropic project proven scientifically is the [overcoming](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/34/marcati_sintomi_societa_futura.htm) of the current social form. In such revolutionary work, the world of carbon and silicon could cooperate for the realisation of the *immediate programme of the revolution* (Forlì meeting of the International Communist Party, 28 December 1952).
