During the Tuesday evening online meeting, we revisited some significant texts produced by the academic world, which represent ideological capitulations of the bourgeoisie before Marxism.

The physicist and Nobel laureate Giorgio Parisi, in the essay *In a Flight of Starlings: the Wonders of Complex Systems*, introduces the reader to the vast topic of complexity; in the subsequent *Le simmetrie nascoste. Perché la fisica è alle radici dell'intelligenza artificiale di oggi e di domani*[^1], he recounts the path that leads from nineteenth-century statistical physics to the theory of complex systems and from there to artificial intelligence. The common thread is the possibility of describing emergent collective phenomena—properties that appear only when an enormous number of agents interact—with the same mathematical tools, whether the “agents” are molecules, magnetic spins, or neurons.

Some aspects addressed by Parisi have already been dealt with by “our” current, which, well before the development of Artificial Intelligence systems, argued for the necessity of a unified science, of a [unitary theory of knowledge](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/16/16_rivista.htm). In recent years there have been thousands of publications on the subject, and among the authors we have taken into consideration are Nello Cristianini (the tetralogy devoted to artificial intelligence), Raymond Kurzweil (*The Singularity Is Near*), Marvin Minsky (*The Society of Mind*), and Alan Turing (*Computing Machinery and Intelligence*).

Since the rapid development of the productive forces is pulling the ground out from under capitalism's feet, accelerating the advent of the historical singularity, the fateful question is put to us with a certain frequency: “what is to be done?” To this end, the letter to the comrades “[Militanti delle rivoluzioni](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/lettere/33a.htm)”[^2] (1996) has been revisited. It is communism that selects its militants and drives them to carry out certain activities rather than others; and it must be understood as a “real movement”, not an ideology or an organisation to which one adheres. A militant of the revolution is not an activist, is not a politician or a trade-union bureaucrat, is not a door-to-door salesman of a particular commodity: he is the one who identifies with the *thread of time*:

> *“The violent sparks that flashed between the rheophores of our dialectics instructed us that a communist and revolutionary militant comrade is one who has been able to forget, to repudiate, to tear away from his mind and from his heart the classification in which he was enrolled by the registry office of this putrescent society, and who sees and mingles himself in the whole of the millenary arc that binds the ancestral, tribal man, fighter against wild beasts, to the member of the future community, fraternal in the joyous harmony of social man.”* “([Considerations on the Party's Organic Activity When the General Situation Is Historically Unfavourable](https://quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/considerations_organic.htm”, 1965))

Bordiga wrote several articles against [activists](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/1952_1970/attivismo.htm) and immediatists, against those who measure the revolution by the yardstick of their own existence. In “Militanti delle rivoluzioni”[^2], individuals are assimilated to molecules, part of material movements that produce collective behaviors. Several scientists have “capitulated” by producing texts in which they demonstrate how all networks share a common order and behave according to the same rules (A.-L. Barabási), how matter has a capacity for self-organisation (S. Kauffman), and how social matter can be analysed just like physical matter (M. Buchanan).

Before establishing “what is to be done”, one must therefore understand what communism (the engine) is and what communists (the instruments) are. The counterrevolution has produced new religions, among them Marxism-Leninism, and also reactions to these, such as the work of *n*+1. The concept of *anti-form*, already present in the “[Layout of Our Work Programme](https://quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/layout_work_programme.htm)” (1946), describes those movements that, before becoming aware of the characteristics of the new order, are forced to break the old social arrangement, causing the emergence of new forms.

According to the school of thought to which we refer, the revolutionary party is a collectivity of men which, although living within capitalist society, is projected into the future. The Occupy Wall Street movement (which had *anti-formist* characteristics) said it was an alien organism calling from the future to rally against capitalism. The militants of the revolutions are captured by a material process that produces effects first in their bellies and then in their heads. Passion comes first, then algebra. It is not a matter of building something within this society, which is collapsing, but of *defending the line of the species' future* ([Property and Capital](https://www.international-communist-party.org/English/Texts/ProprCapital.htm)).

The online meeting continued with commentary on the [election](https://www.avvenire.it/mondo/terremoto-politico-in-germania-lultradestra-di-afd-e-la-prima-forza-in-sassonia-anhalt-cosa-succede-adesso_112717) result in Germany, in the state of Saxony-Anhalt, won by the AfD party with over 44% of the vote.

The country has experienced a phase of relative political and economic stability in recent decades, which is now being jeopardised. The German *establishment* tried everything to eliminate the right-wing AfD party, but only ended up strengthening it. In Germany, particularly in the east, there are problems linked to rising energy costs, factory closures, the flight of young people, and deadly competition with China. The locomotive of Europe discovers that it is no longer such, and society is becoming agitated, finding no adequate answers in the traditional parties. Governmental instability affects more or less all countries. Economic polarization, growing misery, and social hardship lead growing shares of the electorate to prefer the forces that present themselves as anti-system.

In the past we have held a series of meetings on “populism”, analysing recent phenomena such as the *Forconi*[^3], the *Grillini*[^4], the Gilets Jaunes, and even historical experiments such as the *“Fronte dell'uomo qualunque”*[^5] in Italy, Poujadism in France, and Peronism in Argentina. Mass movements that delegitimise parliament and institutions are subject to an *arrow in time*: from anti-political protest one passes, in a decidedly non-linear manner, to anti-system protest. When we speak of the catastrophic trajectory of capitalism, we mean a chaotic process. In complex dynamic systems, especially organic ones, the coexistence of continuity and of “catastrophic” transition is implicit. In the cases just mentioned there is a rupture of continuity, even if the time factor would tell us that the phenomena occurred gradually. Continuity and discontinuity are therefore in a dialectical union, and their identification depends on the type of frame of reference we adopt for the description of phenomena. Contemporary populism registers invariances with that of the last century, but also elements of profound transformation: a typical trait of phase transitions, it today spreads through *social networks* (Trump won also thanks to QAnon) and enters the institutions, increasing chaos instead of limiting it.

The dominant classes struggle to establish a stable order and plunge more or less abruptly into what *Limes* calls “Chaosland”, a process that has now come to lap at the political-economic heart of Europe.

In England, an anti-immigration group linked to the far right [blocked](https://www.rainews.it/video/2026/09/la-protesta-degli-incappucciati-bloccano-il-porto-di-dover-al-grido-di-fermate-i-barconi-11e611f4-4ba9-4572-b32d-886c44ecce07.html) the port of Dover, while clashes between police and anti-migrant protesters occurred in [Portsmouth](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/mondo/2026/09/07/regno-unito-caos-a-portsmouth-anti-migranti-incappucciati-assediano-il-porto_bda8bb0e-e7aa-4b83-83df-5e5993a7ee17.html). From a latent civil war, things can quickly evolve to one fought in the streets.

In the letter to comrades “[Globalizzazione](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/lettere/40a.htm)”[^6] (1999), we wrote that Capital tends to have global effects on the whole of human society from the moment it presented itself on the historical scene as such. So-called globalisation is nothing other than the further development and socialisation of the productive forces which, having reached a certain threshold, have turned against what generated them. These are the dynamics described by Marx in Volume III of *Capital*, ch. 14 (“Counteracting Influences”), that is, the antagonistic influences that counteract or neutralise the action of the general law of the falling rate of profit. In recent decades, European and American industries have delocalised to Asia, especially to China, because the cost of labour was lower. Běijīng, by welcoming Western technologies, made a remarkable leap, developing its own local industry and burning through the stages of capitalist accumulation, becoming a [formidable competitor](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/44/imperialismo-in-salsa-cinese.htm) to the West. In short, capitalism can overcome its contradictions in the immediate term, but only by shifting them into the future and magnifying them. There is no post-American capitalist century on the horizon: America's current crisis represents the historical crisis of senile capitalism.

Among the various problems that the current social form must reckon with is climate change. The British government has published a document (“[Global biodiversity loss, ecosystem collapse and national security](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/696e0eae719d837d69afc7de/National_security_assessment_-_global_biodiversity_loss__ecosystem_collapse_and_national_security.pdf)”) in which it warns of the concrete possibility of the collapse of certain ecosystems (such as coral reefs in Southeast Asia and boreal forests) starting in 2030, and of others (rainforests and mangroves) starting in 2050. All countries are exposed to this risk, both within and beyond their own borders.

The UN secretary-general, António Guterres, has raised the alarm about the [climate situation](https://tg24.sky.it/ambiente/2026/09/03/clima-onu-allarme-el-nino): “El Niño is being supersized before our eyes. The science leaves no room for doubt: the planet is in uncharted waters, and those waters are heating up. The world is in the danger zone of extreme weather”. This issue directly affects States, calling their stability into question. Migration waves, climate disasters, energy blackouts, infrastructure collapse. In the article “[Un modello dinamico di crisi](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/24/modello_dinamico_01.htm)”[^7] we had taken into consideration various parameters, from the planet's biocapacity index to humanity's ecological footprint, reaching the conclusion that the system will collapse around 2030.
