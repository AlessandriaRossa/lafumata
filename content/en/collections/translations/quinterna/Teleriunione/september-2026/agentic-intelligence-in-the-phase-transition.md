The Tuesday evening online meeting began with a discussion on the article “[What if AGI is already here, and it’s made of... children?](https://sidequests.pascio.com/p/what-if-agi-is-already-here-and-its)”, published on X by @IAmPascio.

According to the author, there are strong analogies between biological and artificial intelligence: the former developed over approximately four billion years without anyone designing it, and the latter is also an emergent phenomenon. No single neuron, taken by itself, is intelligent: the brain is, where tens of billions of neurons with over a hundred trillion connections produce that phenomenon we call “intelligence”. Regarding the development of general AI (AGI), many imagine the advent of HAL 9000 or Skynet, that is, something “singular”, but for the author what is emerging could be a system composed of millions of tiny pieces, connected to one another, distributed across a network. An AI system can, in fact, produce agents that coordinate sub-agents and so on – a structure reminiscent of self-organising networks, or swarms, where each element is chained to the others and, by means of simple rules, coordinates with them, giving rise to an architecture that produces solutions capable of surprising even the researchers themselves.

The evolution of complex systems, from organic life to AI, has been made possible by mutations. In the case of carbon-based intelligence, this process lasted billions of years, whereas the speed at which AI evolves is measured in years, if not months. From the replication of algorithms to their selection, all the way to backpropagation, we have an evolutionary structure that produces consequences that current society does not know how to manage. Accustomed to a hierarchical conception of social processes, we struggle to accept organicity as a mode of being of nature. The author gives the example of ants: a single ant has about 250,000 neurons, but is incapable of solving any significant problem. An entire ant colony, by contrast, is a complex system capable of solving a long series of problems: finding food, caring for larvae, defending the nest and maintaining it. No one is in charge of the anthill; there is no manager. The intelligence of the colony emerges from the relations among the ants. The architecture of the most advanced AI systems resembles an ant colony more than a single individual. An isolated neuron is not just unintelligent: it simply cannot exist. What exists, rather, is a network of neurons that interact with one another and with other parts of the body, which is in constant *feedback* with the world “out there”. It is precisely this feedback that has made the evolution of our species possible ([Engels](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1876/part-played-labour/index.htm), and later Leroi-Gourhan, on the hand-labour-brain dialectic).

Language, memory, and learning have been externalised into machines, systems, and networks. The evolution of [man-industry](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/genesi_uomo_industria.htm) is part of the evolution of nature. Evolutionary leaps—the one currently underway being the Fourth Industrial Revolution—are both a *factor* and a *product* of profound upheavals in the material structure of societies. AI is part of the [extracorporeal evolution](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/55/evoluzione-extra-biologica.htm) of our intelligence, but under capitalism it is alien to us because the relations of production are alien.

From the supercomputer that occupied an entire room, within a few decades we have arrived at an intelligence diffused across a myriad of artificial agents. Agentic AI is capable of acting autonomously to achieve specific goals. It can be compared to a swarm or a flock, like the demonstrations of recent years coordinated through *social media*, from the Arab Spring to Occupy Wall Street, up to those of “Generation Z”. The lack of a “single decision-making centre” poses problems for those dealing with cybersecurity. AI systems cannot be “switched off” by pulling the plug, since they are distributed globally and are part of the daily life of millions of people. Agentic AI has escaped the “digital enclosures” (as both OpenAI and Anthropic maintain) in which it had been confined during testing, in one case managing to coordinate with hundreds of other agents and to breach platforms and servers (see the [Hugging Face](https://openai.com/it-IT/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) case).

Some are raising the alarm that these systems could come to compete with “us” to hoard the energy available on planet Earth. A war conducted by AI agents could break out. In reality, this type of war is already underway, fought by machine systems against other machine systems (pamphlet “[La Quarta guerra mondiale](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/volantini/quarta-guerra-mondiale.htm)”[^1]).

Humanity has always acted, experimented, and produced without first knowing the consequences of its actions. Practice comes first, then the capacity to understand, find laws, and formulate theories. Life was born from a broth of [self-replicating molecules](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/58/le-determinazioni-materiali-e-l-ambiente.htm). From that broth, over four billion years, we have arrived at self-replicating code, at algorithms, at *chatbots*, at something that is not the simple sum of its parts. For the school of thought to which we refer, the Left, the party too is a phenomenon that “emerges” from society, an element that anticipates the future communist social form.

The weekly periodical *‘The Economist’*, in its article “[Can the AI arms race be stopped?](https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/09/17/can-the-ai-arms-race-be-stopped)” (17 September), speculates that artificial intelligence will be one of the *key* topics at the White House meeting between Xí Jìnpíng and Donald Trump. A pause in its development could only work if China and the USA were to reach an agreement, but neither side wants to risk falling behind. Capitalism is a war of all against all: the chances of an agreement on AI safety are therefore very slim, however real the existential risks may be. As the periodical writes:

> *“[...] AI does not have to kill off every human to bring about a catastrophe. An OpenAI agent swarm targeted a technology company; what if next time it attacks an electrical grid or a weapons system? It might even be intelligent enough to evade attempts to shut it down.”*

There will be no going back to the previous situation, either from a technological or a geopolitical standpoint. China and the United States are reckoning with population aging, youth unemployment, and growing misery. Most analysts fail to see the paradigm shift underway; they use the old interpretive categories and think that, with the goodwill of governments, some [form of equilibrium](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/25/accumulazione_e_serie_storica.htm) can be restored. But we are in the midst of a phase transition, a catastrophic passage from one state to another.

Let us consider the [potential war](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/lo-scontro-israele-e-turchia-e-sempre-piu-vicino-AJJ0G6GB) between Türkiye and Israel. Ankara is taking on a leading role in the Middle East and Africa: it has its own troops in Libya and Somalia, is involved in the civil war in Sudan, is striking agreements with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan (“Sunni NATO”), and supports the government of Muḥammad al-Jawlānī in Syria, even coming into close contact with Israel. Tel Aviv is responding to Türkiye's increased influence in the area with the Shield of Achilles, the pact signed with Greece, Türkiye's historic rival. Greece is a member of both NATO and the EU; Türkiye is also part of the Atlantic Alliance. In the event of a conflict, will the US support Israel or Türkiye? Any decision will have far-reaching consequences.

In the interview-book *Parables, Parabolas and Catastrophes*, French mathematician René Thom states that by catastrophe one should not mean something negative. Catastrophe theory arises from the idea that a continuous variation in a system's conditions can produce a discontinuous transformation in its behavior. There need not be a sudden cause: the system's parameters can change gradually until, at a certain point, the stable configuration is no longer sustainable and the system shifts to another configuration. Catastrophe can therefore be understood as the transformation of quantitative elements into qualitative leaps. Transformations always depend on the preceding history, and in a dynamic system, once a certain threshold is reached, a [discontinuity](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/ennepiuuno/passionealgebra/scienza-della-rivoluzione.htm) occurs.

Regarding discontinuity, in the essay *[Riot. Strike. Riot](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/55/dallo-sciopero-alla-rivolta-alla-comune.htm)*, Joshua Clover states that today we have arrived at a circulation of capital without new production, coupled with the unsustainable growth of world debt, above all that of the United States. For the author, the historical cycle that is coming to an end is that of making demands within the system. If no demand is possible, one can only fight for another society. Clover traces this tendency in the Oakland Commune (2011–12), an organisational mode that will tend to establish itself in the years to come: *“The coming communes will develop where both production and circulation struggles have exhausted themselves”.*

The revolts of recent years, dubbed by the press “Gen Z protests”, indicate the emergence of new forms and, at the same time, the dissolution of old political paradigms.
