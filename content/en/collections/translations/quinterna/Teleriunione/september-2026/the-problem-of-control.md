The Tuesday evening online meeting began with the reporting of some news concerning the technologies used in modern wars.

According to the most recent estimates, Ukraine produces about four million drones a year, but in 2026 it aims to nearly [double]https://it.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/01/lucraina-annuncia-un-nuovo-meccanismo-per-vendere-armi-ai-paesi-alleati) their production; Russia is proceeding [symmetrically](https://www.analisidifesa.it/2026/01/in-azione-nel-conflitto-in-ucraina-il-nuovo-geran-5-russo/), in turn increasing its own production rates. If Kyïv maintains an advantage in long-range drone attacks, it is nevertheless running out of interceptor missiles, and in some cases the interception rate of Russian ballistic missiles has fallen to nearly zero.

For years, Iran and [Türkiye](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/la-guerra-sudan-arriva-ciad-e-accende-dibattito-ruolo-turchia-AJBOOfw) have also been producing drones on an industrial scale, positioning themselves as suppliers on the market: these are vectors of various types and natures, increasingly difficult to intercept. In recent years, with the advent of autonomous sensors and actuators, unmanned aircraft, and hypersonic missiles, the way war is waged is changing. As in the factory, humans have become appendages of machines.

The First World War was distinguished as a war of attrition (trenches), but also by the emergence of aviation: already in the early years of the XX century, General Giulio Douhet worked on a theory of air supremacy, strategic bombing. In the Second World War, by contrast, the use of armored vehicles and aviation became preponderant, culminating in great naval battles. It was already with the Yom Kippur War (1973), waged by Syria and Egypt against Israel's expansionism, that it became clear how, within a few days, everyone had run out of ammunition; the role of the tank, compared with the previous conflict, emerged diminished. The Fourth World War, the one currently underway, renders the old machines (tank and aircraft carrier) obsolete, replaced by [technologies](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/51/la-sindrome-di-yamamoto.htm) of a new kind.

The United States won the Second World War and was able to transform much of the world in its own image and likeness also because it possessed unprecedented industrial power and an enormous mass of commodities and capitals to export. War is the mirror of the capitalist economic and social structure, and therefore is subjected to its transformations.

The slogan “transform imperialist war into civil war” no longer makes [sense](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/50/wargame.htm) today, given that machines cannot fraternise at the front, while an anonymous ‘gamer’, sitting at a base in Nevada, can launch a missile or a drone directed at any point on the globe. The war phenomenon produces effects on the whole of society, which is swept up in it. Civil war is already underway, and its epicentre is the metropoleis (see the militarisation of police forces). Therefore, the decisive element is *defeatism* against the main enemy, which is one's own bourgeoisie.

In contemporary warfare, the front becomes “hybrid”: an attack can come from any direction, employing a vast array of means. Recently, we have examined in depth the role and importance of logistics and of the supply of weapons and ammunition. The war in Ukraine has demonstrated an enormous consumption of ammunition and supplies on both sides. The conflict between Israel/USA and Iran has highlighted how Iranian drones costing only a few tens of thousands of dollars managed to put the expensive and advanced Israeli air defenses (Iron Dome, David's Sling, Arrow) in crisis.

With the war in Iran, it has become clear how important control of the Straits is: the blockade of Hormuz has produced enormous problems for the Gulf countries, but also for those who live off the raw materials exported from that area. If “[the imperialism of aircraft carriers](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/1952_1970/imperialismo_portaerei.htm)” no longer controls these “chokepoints”, it no longer controls the globe.

The American historian and political scientist Robert Kagan has explained, in several [articles](https://lavocedinewyork.com/america-oggi/2026/05/12/gli-usa-stanno-perdendo-il-golfo-lallarme-di-kagan-sullegemonia-americana/), how the war in Iran represents a strategic defeat for the USA: the Islamic Republic has not been defeated and is positioning itself to become a decisive actor in the Middle East. In the region, even the USA's own historic allies are reckoning with the loss of deterrence—that fear which historically prevented friends and adversaries alike from calling the dollar system into question. A sign of this is the [mutual military assistance](https://www.analisidifesa.it/2026/08/la-strana-alleanza-tra-turchia-pakistan-e-arabia-saudita/) agreement recently signed between Türkiye, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia: Türkiye with power projection across the entire Middle East and North Africa, Pakistan as a nuclear power, Saudi Arabia as an economic power thanks to oil rent. The old alliances no longer hold, so much so that a [clash](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/08/19/raid-israele-siria-turchia-guerra-notizie/8482617/) between Türkiye and Israel seems possible. Türkiye (88 million inhabitants and an army of over 350 thousand men) is one of NATO's most important members, second only to the USA, which should intervene in the event of an attack by Israel.

From the ‘wargame’ perspective, the growing Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian territory (hitting refineries and strategic facilities, as well as logistics centres), even against civilian targets, risk intensifying the Russian offensive, up to the possible use of nuclear weapons. Meanwhile, the government in London is about to launch a [campaign](https://www.iltempo.it/esteri/2026/08/27/news/guerra-russia-gran-bretagna-manuale-burnham-fate-scorta-di-cibo-e-acqua-48965521/) aimed at the citizens, centred on a survival handbook explaining how to behave in the face of attacks of various levels: from cyberattacks capable of paralysing schools and hospitals to actual military attacks. Behind these measures lies the deterioration of relations between London and Moscow, given the United Kingdom's decisive weight in supporting Kyïv. Germany has also stepped up its crackdown on Russian institutions: Berlin reportedly has evidence of Russian intelligence involvement in the [drone](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/mondo/2026/09/01/berlino-attacca-mosca-per-i-droni-di-lipsia.-europa-e-nato-risponderemo_d5e0ef57-89b6-4937-b679-a8050d3a0077.html) attacks on Leipzig airport, and promises measures such as the closure of the Russian consulate in Bonn.

The role of Artificial Intelligence, both in war and in society, is increasing in step with automation in industrial production. This process will lead to the complete autonomy of war, conducted by machine systems. In the essay *Out of Control: The New Biology of Machines, Social Systems, and the Economic World*, Kevin Kelly [describes](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/26/mondo_uomo_industria.htm) the fading of the boundary between the world of the “born” and that of the “made”, and how society will have to recover an organic unity between the two:

> *“The ancient Chinese [...] had the right no-mind about control. Listen to these most modern words from the hand of the mystical pundit Lao Tzu, writing in the Tao Teh King 2,600 years ago: ‘Intelligent control appears as uncontrol or freedom. And for that reason it is genuinely intelligent control. Unintelligent control appears as external domination. And for that reason it is really unintelligent control. Intelligent control exerts influence without appearing to do so. Unintelligent control tries to influence by making a show of force’.*
> 
> *Lao Tzu’s wisdom could be a motto for a gung-ho 21st-century Silicon Valley startup. In an age of smartness and superintelligence, the most intelligent control methods will appear as uncontrol methods. Investing machines with the ability to adapt on their own, to evolve in their own direction, and grow without human oversight is the next great advance in technology. Giving machines freedom is the only way we can have intelligent control.”*

Left in the hands of an “out-of-control” social form, AI becomes a serious problem for the species. The machine, within the capitalistic system, is a factor of further exploitation; in another social form it represents a [factor of liberation](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/filitempo/110_1953_animacavallo.htm) from labour. We are facing a paradigm shift: on one side, the comatose situation in which the system finds itself; on the other, the emergence of a new social form.

If we were to feed AI systems the data on debt growth, the proliferation of war, etc., and ask them to make a projection for the next 5–10 years, even AI would arrive at catastrophic conclusions. [American debt](https://euroborsa.it/debito-usa-conti-2026.aspx) has broken through the 40 trillion dollar mark, a public debt effectively paid by the rest of the world. In August, the USA intervened to [support](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/08/03/stati-uniti-giappone-yen/) Japan, which had plunged into a deep economic and financial crisis—a symptom of the global crisis of capitalism. The real problem of globalisation, of the [interconnection](https://www.economist.com/europe/2026/09/03/america-has-a-whopping-debt-but-europe-has-the-whopping-debt-problem) of markets, emerges clearly in the distorted relationship between the two countries: [Japan](https://www.agi.it/economia/news/2026-08-03/usa-giappone-valuta-yen-38355520/) is among the largest holders of American Treasury securities, while the USA [sold euros](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=84987bNbj5U&t=2004s) to support the yen, in reality supporting itself. When we speak of the crisis of the dollar, we speak not only of the crisis of the USA but also of that of Europe, Japan, etc. The United States must try to resolve a series of interconnected critical issues, from the loss of control over the world to its own internal economic situation.

Capitalism must use ever more energy to solve the problems it generates on a global scale: it is logical that the system, once it reaches a certain threshold, is destined to collapse. Faced with this situation, the age-old question “what is to be done?” arises. We find the answer in our “who we are”: communists adhere to something that really exists, not to a philosophical doctrine, not to one of the many interpretations of the world. Our work is connected to the “Italian” Communist Left, and it is a work of preservation and theoretical elaboration of the heritage left by this current. A work oriented toward the [future](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/46/che-fine-ha-fatto-il-futuro.htm).
