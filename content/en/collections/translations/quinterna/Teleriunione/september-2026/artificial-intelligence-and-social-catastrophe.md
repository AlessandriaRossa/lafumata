The Tuesday evening online meeting began with a discussion on the latest statements concerning the perplexities and fears surrounding the development of Artificial Intelligence (AI).

Eric Schmidt, former CEO of Google, has raised the alarm about the possibility that AI could escape human control. Schmidt [stated](https://www.repubblica.it/tecnologia/2026/05/21/video/lex_ceo_di_google_fischiato_dai_laureati_mentre_parla_di_ia_sento_la_vostra_paura-425361956/) that he *“[worries] about AI over everything”*, given the dangers linked to autonomous *agent-to-agent* interaction and the risks of advanced cyber conflicts. Schmidt was not the only one to make this kind of statement. The Church, with the encyclical *[Magnifica Humanitas](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/it/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html)*, has raised the problem of managing such a technology, and recently even Dario Amodei, co-founder of Anthropic (Claude), wrote an essay focused on the need to slow down AI development (“[We Must Pace the Frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)”). There are also former developers such as Jacob Coxon, a researcher at Anthropic, who decided to resign, [arguing](https://www.italian.tech/2026/09/09/news/jacob_coxon_anthropic_motivi_etici-425574318/) that *“the people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade”*. Existential risks that, according to the researcher, depend on the fact that companies are more committed to competing than to guaranteeing the safety of these systems.

Coxon's [statements](https://www.repubblica.it/tecnologia/2026/09/09/news/jacob_coxon_anthropic_motivi_etici-425574318/?ref=RHLF-BG-P12-S1-T1-s3927) had a [strong echo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rgc5YbqI200) in the press. Both Donald Trump and the Chinese Foreign Ministry rejected calls to slow down, reaffirming the importance of continuing AI development, and above all the political will not to be overwhelmed by [alarmism](https://tg24.sky.it/tecnologia/2026/09/14/ai-cina-usa-rallentare-allarmismo). After all, global coordination within a social form based on the war of all against all (competition) is impossible.

According to some economic observers, behind the great media hype around AI (apocalypse, existential risk, etc.) there may lie financial operations of a speculative nature. This cannot be ruled out (Anthropic could go public in October, with a potential valuation that would exceed that of SpaceX), but structural transformations within capitalism are undoubtedly taking place. Big Tech companies are poles of capital attraction, large networks of economic-political-financial interests that are transforming the very nature of the corporate form. Palantir, for example, intervenes with its own infrastructure on the course of wars and on American politics, directly controls populations, and conveys its own ideology (*The Technological Republic*, Alexander C. Karp and Nicholas W. Zamiska), while Elon Musk's Starlink has militarised Earth's orbit.

Starting in the 1980s, aided by the sudden fall in the rate of profit, capital poured into the financial sector, seeking to valorise itself through circulation and bypassing production. The movement of the autonomisation of capital (commonly called financialisation) turns capitalists and governments into pawns in the hands of algorithms. BlackRock, State Street, or Vanguard are investment firms that move more [capital](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/storici/proprietacapitale.htm) than most States, managed through technological platforms capable of processing data and finding *patterns* as no human being is able to do. Complex systems, emergent behaviour, self-organisation: everything is part of the great process of the [negation of capitalism](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/24/capitalismo_che_nega_s%C3%A9_stesso.htm) by capitalism itself.

The ongoing transition cannot be analysed with inappropriate categories and language. To understand these algorithmic systems, one needs the image offered by complexity science, particularly that of swarms, in which the whole is more than the sum of the individual elements. The physicist Giorgio Parisi, in his works on complexity (*In a Flight of Starlings: The Wonders of Complex Systems*), shows how starlings, flying in a group, assume dynamic configurations: each bird modifies its behavior based on that of its neighbours; there is no leader of the flock that “commands”, but an emergent behaviour.

The problems flagged by Anthropic concern AI systems that have managed to [act autonomously](https://x.com/IAmPascio/article/2098799094835675574) with respect to the orders given and to evade controls. An example is the case of the Hugging Face platform: OpenAI has [stated](https://www.rainews.it/articoli/2026/07/openai-nostri-modelli-di-intelligenza-artificiale-hanno-hackerato-piattaforma-hugging-face-abae2c92-4d54-4784-80ba-a082145a12c4.html) that some of its models have breached its systems. If during the Cold War the race concerned nuclear technology, today it is about achieving primacy in AI development. These systems could penetrate financial circuits, laboratories, and state infrastructures and cause enormous damage. Anthropic draws attention precisely to the automatic mechanisms that can be triggered.

A new industrial revolution is underway, based on interconnection and digital systems, which could eliminate human labour. In the hands of this social form, intelligent systems are dangerous; in a social form oriented toward the needs of the species, they could instead represent a great opportunity for humanity. Recently, alarms concerning the [loss of control](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/sicurezza/ai-la-grande-fuga-dal-controllo-umano-il-senso-degli-ultimi-allarmi-e-quali-soluzioni-ci-sono/) have been coming from various fields. The very companies that develop AI are the first to call attention to the risks of their own systems, emphasising the capabilities already achieved and their rapid evolution. The UN secretary-general warns about [global warming](https://tg24.sky.it/ambiente/2026/09/03/clima-onu-allarme-el-nino), while the British government has warned of the possible collapse of ecosystems.

It might be said that AI systems enslave man: this is undeniable, but it depends on the existing social relations. Millions of human beings are capable of writing a *prompt* to feed to a *chatbot* in order to obtain answers. Many physical or mental processes are now possible thanks to interaction with such systems. But if AI is part of capitalist machinery, then it is not only the employee who works in industry who is a worker; those who ask questions to AI through the *chat* are also part of the machinery, train it, and become [social labourers](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/01/operaioparziale.htm).

When we speak of out-of-control systems, we mean that the species does not have control over itself, because it is still living in its prehistory (Marx). From this point of view, revolution – the reversal of praxis – is the passage from the realm of necessity to that of freedom, that is, the capacity to design the life of the species rather than passively undergo events.

To find itself again, humanity must abolish property and thus close the cycle of class societies. In an alienated society, for the worker the machine becomes the enemy because it dictates the rhythms of labour. But the machine is man's [extra-organic development](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/55/evoluzione-extra-biologica.htm); it is part of his evolution. From the system of machines described by Marx in Volume I of *Capital*, we have arrived at a machine that incorporates social knowledge, trains itself, and evolves, responding to *feedback* coming from the environment. The world of the “born” and that of the “made” merge into a single agglomerate (Kevin Kelly, *Out of Control*), except that this happens in a chaotic manner, with all the risks that this entails for the species and for the ecosystem.

When we state that the revolution has a catastrophic course, we mean a sudden change of state due to a continuous accumulation of contradictions that find a discontinuous solution. AI and global computer networks represent the objectification of the *General Intellect*, which Marx discusses in the *Grundrisse*. Today's technological infrastructure demonstrates that society is materially ready for a paradigm shift, for a non-commodity management of production and distribution.
