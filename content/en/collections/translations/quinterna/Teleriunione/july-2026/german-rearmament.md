The online meeting last Tuesday evening started by drawing from the latest news about Germany.

The texts we have taken into consideration are two: *Limes’* July issue (“[Vogliamo la guerra totale?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uE2rVX2ja2A)”[^1]) – almost entirely dedicated on the German question – and the 1999 preface from our *Notebook* “[Vae victis Germania!](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/storici/vaevictisger.htm)”[^2].

The editorial of *Limes*, “Professione: tedesco”[^3], takes away the logic of German rearmament from its material – economic and social – causes to trace it back to the spirit of a people going through an identity crisis. Conversely, our reference points aren’t ideas, but data, for example those about the production of steel.  In *thread of time* “[Sua maestà l’acciaio](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/filitempo/054_1950_maestacciaio.htm)”[^4] (1950), we read:

> *“War in the capitalist epoch, that is, the most ferocious type of war, is the crisis inevitably produced by the necessity of consuming the produced steel, and by the necessity of struggling for the monopoly right to produce more steel.”*

Even if it is not necessarily “*steel [that] still is the expressive index of the capitalistic mode of production, useful for the comparison of the industrial development between the various countries*”, it still remains a significant indicator.

More than 73% of global steel is produced in Asia. China is by far the most important [producer](https://www.truenumbers.it/produzione-acciaio-il-54-arriva-dalla-cina/): in 2022 it produced more than 1,013 million tons, equal to 54% of global production.

War under capitalism is the continuation of politics and economy through other means. The bourgeoisie, even the most pacifist, can only be subject to [certain pressures](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/51/guerra-in-europa.htm), such as the search for new markets and new opportunities for capital valorisation. In a phase of general asphyxia of the current mode of production, marked by the fall of the rate of profit, competition between capitals pushes towards the search for new investment and expansion areas. Germany and Ukraine have recently signed agreements for the production of drones, employed by Kiev in the war against Russia. For years European countries have been sustaining Ukraine economically through the provision of loans and the supply of armaments.

In April 2026 the German Ministry of Defence published a document about military strategy, *Verantwortung für Europa* (“Responsibility for Europe”), motivated by the increased insecurity determined by the expansion of the crisis and current conflicts, especially in neighbouring countries. The military plan sets itself the goal of not only making Germany the foremost conventional military force of Europe, but also of developing its role as the [driving force](https://www.linkiesta.it/2026/04/germania-difesa-obiettivo-2039/) of the continent. After a first phase (2026-2029) oriented towards the quick enhancement of defensive capabilities and on the consolidation of existent forces, in the second phase (until 2035) a significant military increment in all sectors is expected, for the purpose of assuming a central role in Europe. The third phase, to be completed by 2039, foresees the achievement of technological superiority. The stake would be 335 billion euros worth of investments to be spread throughout 15 years.

In the preface to “Vae victis Germania!”[^2] the history of Germany is recalled, beginning with the analysis of one of Engels’ writings, “To The Working Men of Europe in 1877”. The text highlights the effect of historical humiliations on the military behaviour of the German nation and its army. Defeat and humiliation were healthy carriers of an immediate teaching, but Germany did not manage to transform that experience into a permanent patrimony. Military doctrine registered the material reasons of the defeat, but did not manage to influence neither German society nor its army.

Today Germany aims at building a military force of 460 thousand men, including 200 thousand reservists. A project of this magnitude entails an internal revolution, on the political, economical and cultural plane. But in this world at war, grappling with social turmoil, there is a third wheel capable of wrecking every plan of the bourgeoise: the proletariat, the reserveless army that grows everywhere and has nothing to lose within this society.

German industry – including the war sector – being subordinated to the American one, looks out for areas of autonomy. The Czech Republic, Romania, the Baltic States and the Netherlands fall under the German sphere of influence. Bundeswehr has established formal bilateral relations with the armies of Austria, Norway, Sweden and Estonia.Germany, being the periphery of the US hegemonic bloc, aims to become the centre of Europe, trying to transform its weakness into strength, namely by turning into its own advantage the European insecurity determined by the Russian offensive in Ukraine and by the American “retreat” from NATO. At the risk of its own [decline](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/28/quo_vadis.htm), the German country is forced to move, causing repercussions all around.

The German automotive industry is threatened by Chinese competition: the three car giants, BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen, have [lost](https://www.quattroruote.it/news/mercato/2026/07/13/tedeschi_in_piena_crisi_in_cina-_vendite_in_calo_di_almeno_il_20-.html) a fifth of their sales volumes compared to last year. The *Corriere della Sera* raises the alarm: during the course of 2026 China could arrive at exporting 10 million cars, more than double the Japanese (4 million) and German (3.2 million) exports. In June Beijing recorded a 71% [increase](https://www.corriere.it/economia/finanza/26_luglio_14/la-cina-ha-esportato-oltre-un-milioni-di-automobili-a-giugno-i-rischi-di-un-invasione-in-europa-499ed3f0-10e3-4f86-acc3-6a296f413xlk.shtml) in vehicle exports compared to the same period last year, with a trade surplus of 126 million dollars.

The planet is small, there is not enough space for everyone. The military conversion of German industry is not, therefore, a contingent policy, but an automatic response by the system (“[Volcano of Production or Swamp of the Market?](https://www.international-communist-party.org/English/REPORTS/CrisisTh/54Volcano.htm)”, 1954), as are [American tariffs](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/mondo/2026/07/21/trump-pronto-a-imporre-nuovi-dazi-a-decine-di-paesi-da-questa-settimana_4e293ee1-2297-408f-9702-f6761c974411.html).

Berlin discovers that it is no longer the European locomotive nor the great exporter of cars (and machine systems), and starts to reckon with major Chinese brands that deploy more competitive prices. Volkswagen announced the shut down of various facilities in Germany and the layoff of 50 thousand employees (some sources speculate up to [100,000 redundancies](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/vw-ceo-blume-punta-tagliare-fino-100000-posti-lavoro-prossimi-anni-AI53fzsD)). The German industrial system was oriented around car production. In the last few decades the factory was distributed on the territory through a dense network of suppliers and sub-suppliers, which were entrusted with the specialised production of single components. This productive ecosystem also includes a part of the [industrial structure](https://it.motor1.com/news/408998/quanta-italia-auto-tedesca-fornitori-componenti/) of Northern Italy.

On the works about “[fascist socialisation](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/47/la-grande-socializzazione_1.htm)” (*n*+1, n. 47), we have seen how Volkswagen (literally: car of the people) was the model that, with its realisation, contributed to the transformation of Germany, leading to the construction of highways and bridges, and to the modernisation of the territory, even from the geopolitical point of view. The layoff announced by the major automotive group represents the [crisis](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/56/il-grande-malato-d-europa.htm) of a political and social model.

The attempt of reconverting the automotive industry is not that simple: the strengthening of the war industry entails the employment of automated productive systems. Furthermore, the industrial and economical crisis is accompanied by the political one. The populist party [AFD](https://www.rsi.ch/info/mondo/Germania-alle-origini-del-successo-dell%E2%80%99AfD--2619490.html), according to the polls, hovers at around 30% of preferences; inside it there are pro-Russian components. The prospect of a new corporatist pact, through the fast-paced militarisation of industry and society, appears difficult to implement, also because the young generations don't accept their recruitment in a docile manner. The disintegrating pushes come from outside, but also from within the system.

In the following years in Germany there could be a triggering of catastrophic spirals determined by increasing unemployment, by the rise of populist parties such as AFD, by the recession and by the potential bursting of a financial bubble. This set of factors could set into motion the German proletariat, one of the most organised, which could use “factory discipline” for purposes different from capital valorisation.

From the Middle Eastern front arrive further elements that confirm the picture of general instability, between social turmoil and [war](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/54/la-guerra-e-gia-mondiale.htm). The 17 June memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran proved to be without a future. American bombings have resumed and, with them, the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. There seems to be no way out of hostilities between the two countries. Israel too finds itself in a dead end: Tel Aviv, as pointed out by [Lucio Caracciolo](https://www.limesonline.com/rivista/la-solitudine-di-israele-22024830/), besides having seven open war fronts, has an eighth one within itself. Ultra-orthodox jews do not recognize the State of Israel and refuse to join the army; they are joined by 20% with Arab origins of the Israeli population, exempted from military service. Then there are the Refuseniks, young Israelis that refuse the war for political, ethical or moral reasons. Thousands of Israeli reservists have been on the frontline for three years, they aren’t working and the economy is affected by this. After Iran, the new existential enemy of Israel is represented by Türkiye, which is penetrating into Syria, a country neighbouring the Jewish State. The problem is that Türkiye is the second most powerful army of NATO, after that of the United States.
