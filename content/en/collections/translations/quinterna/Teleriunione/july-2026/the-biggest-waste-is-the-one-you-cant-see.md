The online meeting last Tuesday evening started with a few observations taken from reading the article “[Capitale e teoria dello sciupìo](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/41/capitale_e_teoria_dello_sciupio.htm )” (*n*+1, n. 41).

The bourgeoisie periodically launches moralistic campaigns against resource waste – it gets shocked, for example, by the billion tonnes of food that every year gets [thrown](https://www.legambiente.it/news-storie/lo-spreco-alimentare-una-verita-scomoda/) away in the world – but it is very careful not to go to the root of the problem. Waste, Indeed, does not stem from individual or collective negligence, but from the very way the capitalist means of production functions. 

As we have written in “Capitale e teoria dello sciupìo”, the biggest waste is the invisible one, the one of unpaid labour:

> “Waste is hidden right there: if we have reached such a high relation between labour and surplus labour, this means that the dissipation of human energy in the production cycle alone is equal to at least at that ratio.”

The 5 Stars movement of the early days, on its programme, had intercepted, even though in a confused and populist form, a few of the problems generated by technological development. Their programme talked about the rationalisation of human movement, [automation of labour](https://beppegrillo.it/il-progresso-che-taglia-posti-di-lavoro/) entrusted to robots, a base wage for everyone. That programme was a reflection of the ongoing material transformations of society, but the 5 Stars Movement was not able to be consistent with its own premises and ended up settling into the folds of this society.

Beyond good insights, its essential understanding that every revolutionary transition depends on the struggle between [ways of production](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/ennepiuuno/dottrinamodi.htm), that is low yield forms, destined to disappear, and superior yield forms, destined to to assert themself.

In the production process subjected to capital “something” is generated that is greater than the initial investment. Marx observes how the capitalist, combining means of labour and labour-force, develops an explosive mixture that allows the realization of an increase (M-C-M’). In the simplest capital reproduction, the capitalist consumes all the surplus; in the enlarged reproduction, most of the surplus value is reinvested on the next cycle. At this point, the capitalist could even disappear and be replaced by a waged official or by an algorithm.

With capitalism’s development, the capitalist decides less and less: the one in charge is the capital, that becomes an impersonal net that wraps around everything. The goal of capitalism is accumulation, while bourgeois enjoyment remains a secondary matter. The labour-force is that particular commodity that, once inserted inside the productive process, makes the increase of capital possible. But the actual useful human labour for the species is only a small quota compared to the total labour effectively employed by capitalism. In future society, overcome the social division of labour, the productive process will become a metabolic process and all will be time for living.

In our reference text, we find it written:

> *”The excess and squander of social labour of the proletarian class, compared to the quantity of useful products to the consumption, gives a passive rate dozens of times worse than the relationship that exists for the individual wage-earner between unpaid and paid labor, or the rate of surplus value”* (ICP, [Proprietà e Capitale](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/storici/proprietacapitale.htm), 1948).

Oggi non si producono beni utili al soddisfacimento delle esigenze della specie umana, ma alle necessità del capitale. Nel caso, ad esempio, del trasporto umano, da un punto di vista della specie non ha senso continuare a produrre veicoli ultra-dissipativi del peso di centinaia di kg per trasportare, per poche ore al giorno, una persona che mediamente ne pesa 80. La società attuale dissipa risorse nella competizione, mentre è dimostrato che è più vantaggiosa la cooperazione ("Operaio parziale e piano di produzione"). Anche la guerra è uno spreco immane di energie sociali, di vite e di mezzi. Lo sperpero è riscontrabile anche nell'esistenza degli apparati utili al funzionamento dell'attuale modo di produzione, dalle forze di polizia alla burocrazia di Stato. Sul fenomeno della disoccupazione si è formato un enorme business, che comprende sindacati, enti bilaterali, agenzie interinali, ecc. Si arriva così alla famiglia, unità minima che si occupa di consumare la merce e di ripartire il risparmio (eredità). Un altro esempio di dissipazione è rappresentato dalle giovani generazioni, che avrebbero tempo ed energia da impiegare per la comunità umana e invece sono intrappolate in una "vita senza senso", alla ricerca di un lavoro che non c'è, preda di manie consumistiche e atteggiamenti antisociali e autodistruttivi. Nelle società antiche, i giovani erano parte essenziale; in quella attuale, molti sono forza lavoro inutilizzabile. 

Nowadays, goods are not produced to satisfy the needs of the human species, but the necessities of capital. In the case of human transport, for example, from the perspective of the species it makes no sense to continue producing ultra-dissipative vehicles weighing hundreds of kilograms in order to transport, for a few hours a day, a person who on average weighs 80kg. Modern society dissipates resources in competition, while it has been demonstrated that cooperation is more advantageous ("[Operaio parziale e piano di produzione](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/01/operaioparziale.htm)"[^1]). Even war is an immense waste of social energies, lives, and means. Waste is also found in the existence of apparatus necessary for the functioning of the current mode of production, from police forces to State bureaucracy. An enormous *business* has formed around the phenomenon of unemployment, including trade unions, bilateral organisations, temporary employment agencies, etc. This leads to the family, the smallest unit that deals with consuming commodities and distributing savings (inheritance). Another example of dissipation is represented by the younger generations, who would have time and energy that could be employed for the human community but are instead trapped in a "meaningless life", searching for a job that does not exist, prey to consumerist manias and antisocial and self-destructive attitudes. In ancient societies, the young were an essential part; in the current one, many are unusable labour power.

In the face of a system that offers no prospects, practices refusing work and consumerism are spreading, generating alternative environments in which that refusal is consciously embraced as a life choice. *[Downshifters](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/24/movimento_semplicita.htm)*, a typically American current, are those who prefer to work little, living in campers or trailers and reducing unnecessary consumption in order to dedicate more time to themselves. Similar tendencies have emerged in China with "Tǎng Píng" (literally, "lying flat"). The phenomenon of the refusal of work is global and has been addressed in various articles and essays; researcher Francesca Coin, in her book *The Great Resignation. The New Refusal of Work*, states that freeing life from work is an existential urgency: we would add that it is a [historical necessity](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/54/tendenza-antiwork.htm).

The development of the social productive force [dissolves](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/necessarie_dissoluzioni.htm) social forms that have become obsolete, and among these is certainly that of work understood as a social elevator. On this subject, two recent books have been published which we would like to point out: the first, *I Deliver Parcels in Beijing* (Hú Ānyān), is the autobiography of a Chinese worker that documents exploitation, precariousness, and the real subsumption of labour under capital in the sector of goods circulation. The second, *Factory Girls* (Leslie T. Chang), deals with the condition of female labour in China, between exhausting shifts, minimum wages, and the little time left over from work.

The immense waste of energy condemns capitalism not from a moral standpoint, but from a thermodynamic one.

The productive forces are so pregnant with future that society n, in comparison, appears immediately obsolete. The Internet, artificial intelligence, the enormous productive capacity of almost fully-automated factories are results that the entire species has come to. The interaction between humans, tools, and networks already today enables the ["reversal of praxis"](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/rovesciamento_prassi.htm), as described in "Elementi della transizione rivoluzionaria come manifesto politico"[^2], on the homepage of our website.

The theme of dissipation is central to all of the Left's work, starting from the texts we have collected in the notebook *[Scienza economica marxista come programma rivoluzionario](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/storici/scienzeconom.htm)*[^3]. But Engels already addresses the topic in the [two speeches](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/marx/works/1845/02/15.htm) he gave in Elberfeld in 1845, in which he demonstrates that the greatest waste is not that of major frauds or the high salaries of politicians, but rather that internal to the material structure of society:

> *From the economic point of view the present arrangement of society is surely the most irrational and unpractical we can possibly conceive. The opposition of interests results in a great amount of labour power being utilised in a way from which society gains nothing, and in a substantial amount of capital being unnecessarily lost without reproducing itself.*

[Energy dissipation](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/31/massimo_di_entropia.htm) is a phenomenon linked to the anarchy of the capitalistic system, which cannot make rational use of neither human labour or the [resources](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/31/energia_domani.htm) offered by the planet (see "ecological footprint"). According to environmentalism, one should fight for a green capitalism – but capitalism is this, there cannot be another one. For the economist Jeremy Rifkin (*Entropy*), this society's waste is already visible in the production of a packaged brioche (chemical industry, steel industry, construction, transport, etc.): tens of thousands of kilocalories are devoured by the productive process to put on the table a product that is certainly not a health tonic – thus triggering a further cycle: medical clinics, hospitals, medicines, etc. Rifkin analyses every economy as a system of production/reproduction subject to the second law of thermodynamics, that is, a dissipative energy system.

Some researchers – think, for example, of the technocrats in the 1930s – arrived at the necessity of accounting in terms of energy rather than in terms of value, and this is an “ideological capitulation of the bourgeoisie before Marxism.”

In recent years, global warming has become one of the great emergencies that society is facing: the release into the atmosphere of thousands of tons of carbon dioxide (CO₂), nitrogen, and other microparticles produced by industry or by otherwise senseless human activity in general has wrapped planet Earth in a film of poisons. To reverse this trend, one would need to lower the temperature of the system, intervening above all in the way production is carried out. But capitalism is interested only in profit: a recent example is the planning of enormous [data centres](https://www.milanotoday.it/attualita/manifestazione-data-center-lacchiarella.html) to power artificial intelligence systems, which, in order to be cooled, require enormous quantities of water (taken away from human needs), and ever-growing electricity consumption.

In closing the meeting, we mentioned the last issue of *Limes*, titled "[Vogliamo la guerra totale?](https://www.limesonline.com/articoli/vogliamo-la-guerra-totale-il-numero-626-di-limes-22303107/)"[^4] and focused of German rearmament. The major countries are equipping themselves with [drones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MhmUyjn8bXg&t=406s), artificial intelligence systems, and hypersonic missiles, diverting resources and social energy towards the war industry. Berlin has published a strategic document, *Verantwortung für Europa* (“Responsibility for Europe”), in which it announces the objective of turning Germany into the foremost [military power](https://formiche.net/2026/04/germania-difesa-2039/) in Europe by 2039, motivating this choice with the USA’s previously disclosed disengagement from the Old Continent and with the menace represented by Russia. For an industrial country like [Germany](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=olEiwxw9dcY), whose crown jewel was the [car industry](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/vw-ceo-blume-punta-tagliare-fino-100000-posti-lavoro-prossimi-anni-AI53fzsD), this turn comes at a time when it’s facing the growing competition of Chinese electric car juggernauts: [Berlin](https://www.startmag.it/spazio-e-difesa/come-cambiera-la-difesa-europea-con-la-nuova-strategia-militare-tedesca/)’s solution is to convert part of its civilian production—beginning precisely with the car industry—to the military sector. It remains to be seen how this great transformation will be possible, but it’s significant that all major powers are talking about rearmament.
