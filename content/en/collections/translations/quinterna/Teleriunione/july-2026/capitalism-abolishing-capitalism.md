The online meeting of last Tuesday evening started with some remarks on the [interview](https://www.economist.com/insider/the-insider/an-interview-with-elon-musk) that Elon Musk released last 23 July to Zanny Minton Beddoes, director of *[The Economist](https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/07/23/should-you-be-afraid-of-elon-musk)* – the first major public comment after the SpaceX stock market listing (12 June), which had made him the first trillionaire in history.

Musk speaks once again about Artificial Intelligence (AI), [claiming](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/07/27/musk-intelligenza-artificiale-robot-economist-notizie/8460198/) that in the next ten years robots and AI systems will replace the majority of human labour. This would open an epoch of “abundance” in which work will become optional, while money, tied by its nature to the scarcity of resources, will lose meaning.

These are positions already dealt with in the article “[Ideologie di un capitalismo che nega sé stesso](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/57/ideologie-di-un-capitalismo-che-nega-se-stesso.htm)”[^1] (*n*+1, n. 57), where we had reviewed the various currents – accelerationist, longtermist, transhumanist, etc. – mostly tied, if not geographically at least culturally, to the environment of Silicon Valley. In the interview Musk makes an explicit reference to the *[Culture Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_series)*, a collection of science fiction novels by Iain Banks, who imagines a post-scarcity space society where men and robots live together without labour nor money. It is not a random reference: this is the same cultural path reconstructed by Fabio Chiusi in the essay *L’uomo che vuole risolvere il futuro. Critica ideologica di Elon Musk (2023)*[^2], tied to the heritage of his maternal grandfather, member of the 1930s’ Canadian section of Technocracy Incorporated – the movement that wanted to replace democratic politics with a government of engineers, the only ones capable of “scientifically” managing the production and distribution of goods. Others identify in some of Musk’s declarations a few references to late 19th-century Russian Cosmism, a mystical-scientific current that viewed the conquest of space and overcoming of death as the fulfillment of human evolution, and from which descends, through other paths, the transhumanism prevalent today in Silicon Valley and beyond. Hence Musk’s “technological solutionism”, that is, the idea that historical and social problems – work, scarcity, death itself – are technical problems, solvable through engineering bypassing politics as the mediation between opposing interests. Consistently with this [formulation](https://www.ilfoglio.it/il-foglio-ai/2026/07/28/news/la-versione-di-elon-musk--403340), Musk proposed an institution called “universal high income”, a sort of universal wage meant to redistribute wealth.

In China as well the debate on AI and its social consequences is pressing on. The economist Cài Fǎng, one of the most authoritative Chinese scholars of the labour economy, published on the *Study Times* (and later on *Qiúshì*, theoretical magazine of the Central Committee of the CPC) a text with the title “[Responding Proactively to AI’s Impact on Employment](https://www.qstheory.cn/20260619/4abab8e31a95432aa919864d426fc10d/c.html)”, in which he proposes to overcome the minimum wage in favour of a “living wage”, alongside a universal basic income and a non-contribution-based pension for all the elderly, together with a new social responsibility of firms in the redistribution of productivity gains generated by AI.

Topics that until a few years ago where the prerogative of so-called extremists (“[Diritto al lavoro o libertà dal lavoro salariato?](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/volantini/dirittolavoro.htm)”[^3]) are today at the center of bourgeois debates, in the West as in the East: it is no longer a marginal minority that speaks about ending work and giving wages to the unemployed, but a figure of the calibre of Elon Musk, while in China the economist Cài Fǎng – a prominent figure, already member of the central bank's monetary policy committee – proposes its application on the national scale. Both of them, by doing so, involuntarily show that capitalism negates itself.

It is the same contradiction in which capitalism writhes, stretched between the development of productive forces and the defence of the current property relations. A contradiction that the maturation of capitalism does not solve but sharpens, to the point – described by Lenin (*Imperialism*) – where the shell no longer fits its content. Communism isn’t an ideology but a real movement which, sooner or later, everyone is forced to reckon with.

Musk, in the interview, even calls for a collaboration between China and the United States to govern AI’s development, a hope which, however, inevitably clashes with the laws of capitalist development – competition, unequal development, etc. – that render it structurally unfeasible. Conversely to what was stated by Karl Kautsky in 1914 (capitalists of the world, unite!) a [super-imperialism](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/06/superimperialismo.htm) capable of overcoming the competition between national capitals is not possible.

Alongside those that promise a future of abundance, there are those who prepare the tools for the dispute. We’re referencing the galaxy of entrepreneurs tied to Peter Thiel: Alex Karp, CEO of Palantir, that with Nicholas Zamiska signed the book *The Technological Republic*, summarised in a 22-point [manifesto](https://www.infodata.ilsole24ore.com/2026/04/24/palantir-repubblica-tecnologica-22-punti-hard-power-ai-guerra/) published by Palantir on social media last April. The text argues that Silicon Valley has a moral debt towards America and that deterrence, today, is played not only on the nuclear but above all on the AI field, whose development must be put at the service of US technological supremacy. Palmer Luckey, founder of Anduril (a firm of autonomous weapon systems), shares similar views. These technologies are already employed in Ukraine, in Gaza and in ICE operations. Joe Biden, before leaving the White House, to trip Donal Trump up, recalled Dwight Eisenhower’s famous speech (1961) on the military-industrial complex to warn about the potential threat of a technological oligarchy.

> *“Today, an oligarchy is taking shape in America of extreme wealth, power, and influence”*, Biden declared, stating that he was *“concerned about the potential rise of a tech-industrial complex that could pose real dangers for our country as well”*.

It should be noted that the interests of these “giga-capitalists” do not always converge: Thiel  largely depends on US government commissions, Musk instead has relevant interests also in China.

AI development is beginning to have effects also on the scientific world, in particular in the field of mathematical studies. Lean, a *proof assistant* in development since 2013 as an *open source* project, is a formal language that allows the translation of mathematical enunciations and demonstrations into a sequence of logical passages, certainly provable by a control kernel; if the demonstration is formally correct, the system convalidates it step by step; if one of the passages isn’t proved, it gets rejected. With Lean 3, published in 2017, took form [Mathlib](https://mathlib.org/), a [collaborative](https://leanprover-community.github.io/lean3/) library of formalised mathematics that today counts hundreds of collaborators. In the last two years various AI laboratories have developed systems capable of proposing mathematical demonstrations that were then verified precisely by Lean: in 2024 one of these has reached the silver-medal level at the International Mathematical Olympiad, while in 2025 others have touched the golden-medal level. Thus, a limited field of academic positions, often defended with various gimmicks, begins to waver, and the phenomenon is spreading also to physics.

We would surely not be able to approach these topics if we did not know that what we are passing through is a *phase transition*: it is not single aspects of capitalism that are mutating, but something deeper, that concerns the system as a whole. This is what explains the convergence, otherwise surprising, between environments so distant: if we talk about the end of wage labour in the theoretical furrow of the Communist Left, the same topics are now also discussed by the bourgeoise, pushed, however, by an opposite path – that of the development reached by productive forces, which is challenging their world, their ideologies. Musk, in this sense, makes himself the interpreter – against his will – of a movement that transcends him. Today we are faced with a mixture of theories, even extremely contradictory ones, because we are in an epoch of change, where everything is discombobulated.

On news headlines, two news articles confirm the picture of increasing social instability. In Germany the experts commission in charge of the [pension reform](https://www1.wdr.de/radio/cosmo/sprachen/italiano/italiani-germania/pensioni-germania-riforma-commissione-100.html) presented to chancellor Friedrich Merz a [proposal](https://it.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/02/germania-il-cancelliere-tedesco-merz-vara-le-riforme-sulla-patrimoniale-e-sulle-pensioni) that imposes more flexibility on the job market (longer Sunday hours) and the abolition of the sickness certificate obtained by phone, the expansion of the stalls of taxpayers and the institution of a capitalised supplementary pension fund: more years of work, a new supplementary public pension and a farewell to retirement age at 63, without penalisation.

In India, successo was seen by the [mobilisation](https://www.chicago86.org/lotte-in-corso/asia/india/1458-i-giovani-indiani-scendono-in-strada) of the *Cockroach Janta Party*, the juvenile movement born on social media after a scandal about leaks from the Medical school national entrance exams, and rapidly grown after the president of the Indian Supreme Court, Surya Kant, defined the young unemployed of the country as “cockroaches”. After weeks of demonstrations, hunger strikes and police charges in Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore and Kolkata, the minister of education Dharmendra Pradhan resigned last 25 July, accepting the main demand of the movement. As was the case with the juvenile mobilisations in Nepal, Indonesia and elsewhere (“The Gen Z protests”) –  that, beyond the official reasons, arise from a background of increasing misery and youth unemployment –  what’s striking is the [speed](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/07/27/scarafaggi-minaccia-narendra-modi/) with which the demonstration has spread and gained political weight, above all thanks to the capacity of using the tools today available to spread its own demands. Let’s imagine what an *antiform* movement on a global scale could do.

When we talk about the increasing misery, about the jamming of capital valorisation mechanisms, about political and economic polarisation, we refer precisely to these events. Capitalism is facing many unresolved and unresolvable problems, and Musk, with all his contradictions, registers its symptoms in his own way.

We still are in a preparation phase: on one side we have the “cockroach” movement, on the other side the *One Piece* manga flags raised at other protests in Asia. They are demonstrations that do not have a precise class connotation yet, but it is precisely when the social molecules begin to fibrillate that we catch a glimpse of a tendency that will become clearer and will be oriented by an attractor located in the future (“[Schema of the Reversal of Praxis](https://www.quinterna.org/lingue/english/historical_en/theory_and_action.htm)”). This tendency obviously will not have all the characteristics and the aesthetics of the 1920s: the world has changed and the [symptoms of future society](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/34/marcati_sintomi_societa_futura.htm) are getting sharper. To all this, the conditions of the reserverless contribute: *gig-workers* exist in Italy as well as in China and in Nepal.
