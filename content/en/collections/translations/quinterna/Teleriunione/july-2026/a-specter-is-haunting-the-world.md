The online meeting last Tuesday evening focused on an analysis of the economic and social situation of China and the United States, looking for invariances that are common to both countries.

[Cài Fǎng](https://lowdown.today/e/cai-fang/), former vice president of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, President of the Chinese Association of Labour Economics, and member of the World Bank’s High-Level Advisory Council on Jobs, in his recent book *New Trends in China's Employment: How Artificial Intelligence Is Reshaping the Labour Market* examines the potentially disruptive impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on the Chinese job market, theorising the detachment of wages from work performance. In his other public speeches he has also proposed the introduction of a “[living wage](https://ilmanifesto.it/dal-salario-minimo-al-salario-vitale)”, the basic income and non-contributory pension.

Choramedia, the *podcast company*, has dedicated an episode to the topic (“[China wants [the/a] livinf wage: what is really changing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5faIpaQQ2I)”), underlining its importance. When tens of millions of workers have to download an *app* to find a job with the hope of getting connected to someby a platform, it means that the Asian giant grapples with the same aches of this ailing capitalism that western countries do. The situation isn’t any better in India, where thousands of youths have gathered in New Delhi to demonstrate against Narendra Modi’s government. The mobilisation was organised by the “Cockroach Janata Party”, a web-born satirical movement that complains about high juvenile unemployment and rampant corruption, demanding  a reform of the education system.

Chinese society rests on an unspoken rule: in exchange for the promise of continuing improvement in living conditions,  the population accepts the authoritarian guidance of the Party-State. Now, this rule has begun to break, as the prospects of social advancement have frozen: millions of university students enter the job market every year and end up becoming *[gig-workers](https://www.chicago86.org/lotte-in-corso/asia/cina/1429-il-grande-reggimento-cinese-dell-esercito-globale-dei-gig-workers)*. Labour via platforms, precarious and discontinuous, no longer concerns just *food delivery*, but also the industrial sector. It is against this backdrop that president Xí Jìnpíng’s slogan of "[Shared Prosperity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gl3AtcXlVIU)"  emerges.

Technological unemployment, AI, and economic polarisation are closely concatenated phenomena. Cài Fǎng argues that high industrial autonomisation may cause a long term *shock* and that juvenile unemployment could reach a nearly lryhardly uncontrollable state. The Chinese social structure bears characteristics different from the western ones: China has acceleratedfast-tracked the process of capitalist development, becoming the “world’s factory”, bringing with this the typical problems of modern capitalism, even still maintaining a particular agrarian structure made of almost 200 million small agricultural units (see the Chinese debate about the [Hùkǒu](https://www.china-files.com/lhukou-e-il-controllo-sociale/) system).

The Chinese youth and the American youth share the same problems: both in major powers that challenge each other for economic primacy, military superiority, and supremacy in the AI field. In both countries where there is an elephantine State apparatus, sophisticated population control techniques, and the reserveless masses.

The rates of youth unemployment are so worrying to the extent that the Chinese State is being pushed into considering the introduction of some sort of unemployment benefit. One must reckon with the "real movement," and this applies to anyone governing a country. Just a few years ago in China there emerged the “Tǎng Píng” movement: youths  that refused the exhausting work culture and chose to live a more relaxed life, with reduced consumption and few economic resources. In the USA, on the other hand, was born the “AntiWork” subreddit, around which has developed an informal movement to which millions of young and oldnon young people have adhered.

The working laws of capitalism make it such that the economic and social contradictions cannot be solved while remaining within the current social form.

Lucio Caracciolo, *Limes*’ director, in the article “[Marx, Trump e il nuovo spettro del comunismo in America](https://www.limesonline.com/rubriche/il-punto/comunismo-socialismo-america-usa-paura-rossa-red-scare-karl-marx-prima-internazionale-lincoln-donald-trump-mamdani-ocasio-co-22295555/)”[^1], analyses the recent statements of the American president about the celebrations of the 250 years of the United States’ Declaration of Independence. Trump lashed out against the threat represented by communists, referencing the growth, inside the Democratic Party, of the leftist faction, that of the socialist democrats who Bernie Sanders, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and the new mayor of New York, Zohran Mamdani, are part of. Trump [stated](https://tg24.sky.it/mondo/2026/07/04/4-luglio-trump-discorso) that *"you can be loyal to Karl Marx or you can be loyal to America. You can be a communist or you can be a patriot. You cannot be both."*.

Caracciolo lists a series of polls from which emerges a return of socialism in the USA: 4 Americans out of 10, and 5 out of 10 among those under 35, consider socialism a good thing. A [split](https://www.panorama.it/attualita/esteri/midterm-usa-leffetto-mamdani-preoccupa-le-alte-sfere-del-partito-democratico) is unraveling within the Democratic Party: one side is attempting to sport a new look, winking at Neo-Keynesianism and wealth redistribution policies, in an attempt to intercept the malcontent in some of its base. Accord to *Limes*’ director, the formation of a blue MAGA is possible, a mirror image of Trump’s red MAGA.

Beyond the real results that the American democrats will be able to achieve in the next elections, the distinctive character of “Generation Z” consists of ignoring the old political structures, as the MAGA movement has moved beyond the bounds of the Republican Party. In the global uprisings of recent years we have seen the manifestation on the Web of forms of swarm intelligence. The true spectre of Communism is not  shown by the American Democratic Party’s left[ wing?], but by the dissolution of the party-apparatus, of syncretic programmes, of third-internationalist language, and of the reformist approach (“[Necessarie dissoluzioni](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/necessarie_dissoluzioni.htm)”)[^2].

As was [pointed out](https://europeanpulse.eu/it/the-outlook/the-rise-of-generation-z-socialism/) by *The Economist* on 9 June (“How to fight back against Gen-Z socialism”), the American “Gen Z” is arriving closer to Socialism due to the material conditions that it finds itself living in: lack of accommodation[s?], uncontrollable rent, increasing inflation, and rising levels of college debt.

The economist and sociologist Werner Sombart, in the 1906 essay *Why Is There No Socialism in the United States?*claimed that socialists had not taken hold in the USA because [American capitalism](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/47/la-grande-socializzazione_1.htm) had developed in a state of resource abundance that guaranteed workers wages higher than their European counterparts’, and in a cultural context characterised by the frontier myth and the availability of new spaces to conquer. Sombart, however, warned that, once these spaces and myths were spent, the conditions that until then had impeded the rise of Socialism could be transformed into their opposite.

From the writings of Marx and those of the [Communist] Left, we know that capitalism contains the elements that will lead to its collapse; we can thus affirm its potential non-existence (“[Property and Capital](https://www.international-communist-party.org/English/Texts/ProprCapital.htm)”). Per Lenin, economic and private-property relations constitute a shell which no longer fits its contents.

Such observations find confirmation today through the development reached by the productive forces. Look at, for example, the *[grokking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grokking_(machine_learning))* phenomenon: in practice it is observed that, in training an artificial neutral network on small sets of data related to algorithmic problems, initially it learns by memorising the solutions present in the *dataset*. Subsequently, after many training cycles during which the performances don’t show significant improvement, a sudden change occurs: the model gains the ability of generalisation, becoming able to find correct solutions even on data that it had never encountered before. In other words, the neural network goes from mnemonic learning to generalisation not through a linear process, but by means of a sudden transition akin to a phase transition. There are various proceedings to individuate the approach of *grokking*, but the mechanisms that determine the passage from one phase to another have yet to be explained definitively, only hypotheses have been formulated.

Even war, being ever more integrated into the industrial and technological system, is undergoing a deep transformation. Contemporary war, conducted with drones, radars, and AI systems, involves the [whole society](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/51/wargame-parte-seconda.htm), from universities to firms. *Big Tech* produces technologies intended for both military and police agencies. Defeatism today is all-encompassing, or it doesn't exist at all. ICE operations against migrants (based on Palantir’s *softwares*) use the same AI systems employed for the military operations carried out in the Gaza Strip.

The lightness of *software* presupposes the heaviness of *hardware*. AI needs enormous calculation centres to work, a problem that all major Countries must face: the five *datacentres* [under construction](https://www.corriere.it/economia/energie/26_giugno_11/data-center-lombardia-milano-consumi-8fa5d94b-b97d-4441-b4dd-e41cd3897xlk.shtml) south of Milan, for example, will consume as much energy as the whole city of Bologna. This infrastructure requires an ever-increasing quantity of energy and water to work; they are intended not only for business, but also for war and population control. The degree of dissipation achieved by this social form has no equal in history (“[Capitale e teoria dello sciupio](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/41/capitale_e_teoria_dello_sciupio.htm)”)[^3].
