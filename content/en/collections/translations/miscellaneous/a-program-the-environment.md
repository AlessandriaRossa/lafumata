We have long fought against the opinion of those who wish to give a cultural direction to the socialist youth movement. We have affirmed that such a direction can correspond to a work of democratic preparation, but not of revolutionary preparation.

Our fundamental theoretical argument has always been that political opinions are the result not of abstract ideas or philosophical and scientific enlightenments, but of the environment in which one lives and the immediate necessities of such an environment. Our thesis is materialistic – in the style of Charles Marx – opposed to idealist conceptions of every nature and very little shaken by bourgeois and non-bourgeois revisionism. It may not be accepted by all comrades, but we persist in believing that outside of it there is no possibility of giving a basis to the socialist bulwark and mentality. Above all, we believe that facts are mounting further and further, when one knows how to examine them outside of the falsifications of bourgeois culture and without transcending to useless intellectual skirmishes.

The proletarian environment, from which socialism rises spontaneously, is – as every social environment – determined and increased by commonality of economical interests. When acknowledging this fundamental truth, and making it into a constant guide for the resolution of every political and social problem, we have never dreamed of negating the existence of neither “feelings” or “idealities”, using this word to mean the consciousness of a real goal to reach in everyone’s interest, but which could, at certain moments of action, demand the sacrifice of some (we often repeat ourselves, but with good reason). On the contrary, we see political opinion more as a matter of “feeling” rather than a product of philosophical and scientific culture. Only we put at the basis of socialist feeling the economic conditions, instead of pretending that socialism descends to dealing with the economic problem as an effect of the “mutual instinct of justice”, etc.

We believe—and this is the focal point!—that the mistakes, weaknesses and betrayal of any comrade shouldn’t be attributed to cultural deficiencies, but to having little by little moved away from the environment and to having lost the socialist “feeling”. Only priests can believe in “conversions”, not us.

So too, to the fact that errors are committed not by rappresentative individuals but by groups of workers, one will never remedy the situation with culture, if those groups aren’t provided with the atmosphere of the socialist environment.

The “Culturalists” are worried about the fact that certain categories of workers, having conquered some privileges, stop being socialists in the true sense of the word, and betray the class struggle. They wish to remedy this most deplorable – but unfortunately logical – situation with “culture”. We believe instead that it’s necessary to avoid the formation of these privileged environments and bring workers in close proximity with the other categories, make them live outside their local group, ensuring that they understand that it is necessary to make sacrifices not only for their own trade union, but for all of their worker comrades exploited by the bourgeoisie. This is not a work of culture, but of "environment formation”. This work must be reserved to the Socialist Party, and this is why we put the *party’s revolutionary mission way above that of the unions*, regardless of any acolyte to which the secretaries of the latter belong.

Since they would like to remedy defections with a work of culture, let us examine a bit closer these painful phenomena. We will begin by making a distinction between socialist workers and socialist “intellectuals”

The worker becomes as socialist when they start considering their position as victim not in an isolated way, but together with their worker comrades. This  – we have said so many times! –  is the consequence of their state of economic discomfort for which the self-preservation instinct makes them look for a remedy. Upon doing these efforts to improve themselves, the worker ends up seeing that it's necessary to strike at the root of the current economic regime, and in order to do that the struggle must be brought to the political ground, directing it against the present institutions.

It's clear that this same self-preservation instinct that pushed them on this path, holds them back at the decisive moment of revolutionary action, and many times the worker ends up adapting to the current condition, out of fear of risking too much and getting a worse deal. But when certain particular economic conditions exacerbate their rebel feelings, then they don't hesitate anymore and throw themselves onto the revolutionary struggle.

Now the Socialist Party, aiming to hasten such a process, wants to convince the worker of the necessity of carrying out that struggle, the only possible solution to the social problem in the interest of the proletariat. The worker who is solidly convinced of this is a good socialist. What, then, will be the *method* to carry out such a conviction? That of theoretical demonstration, of culture? We then should wait many more centuries to “prepare” the proletariat!

No, for god's sake, the way of propaganda isn’t theory, but feeling, since this is the spontaneous reflection of the material needs in the nervous system of men.

It is necessary, if we want to win the worker’s egoistical reluctance, to show them the conditions of their kind, to bring them in an environment that speaks to them about the "class" and its future. Under the influence of such an environment they will not run the risk of becoming a renegade. And that this isn’t a work of culture is proved by the case of the intellectuals that “recant” with great ease, despite the theoretical solidity of their ideas, which workers could certainly never reach.
But the case of intellectuals is quite different. They come from a non-socialist environment, by chance, by instinct maybe, more often for having bumped into a sharp corner of the environment they leave – almost never in [the] conscious bad faith to make a political pedestal for themselves, as this comes afterwards.

Real conviction, in general, forms afterwards, in contact with the workers' environment, due to the comparison with the one they left… *Political opinion isn’t an attitude of thought*, let’s repeat it at the cost of being stoned by idealists, culturists, “Philosophy” or “Science” maniacs. I know many that in theoretical knowledge are socialists and in politics are reactionaries. Are there, perhaps, a few cases of the…viceversa! However, since both the intellectual and the worker believe, very often, in the political superiority of the most cultured man, they end up finding themselves in two distinct planes, and the worker gets used to believing that the intellectual is a superior being, with immensely greater possibility of action… they end up turning him into an idol, and meanwhile they bring him out of the workers’ environment. Thus starts the logical parable of the bourgeois socialists, reabsorbed by bourgeois society. It is an almost necessary process: the proletariat steals from the bourgeoisie some revolutionary, advanced elements, and exploits them against it until the bourgeoisie manages to bring them back into its ranks. It is a continuous transfer that wouldn’t cause much damage to socialism if those intellectuals, by leaving, wouldn’t leave behind a legacy of personalistic admiration among the workers. The enemy we face in these phenomena, the demiurge of worker and non-worker defections from our ranks, is always the same: its name is “individualism”. It is the reflection of the environment of bourgeois society. It has its roots in the economic regime of private property and competition. It is an enemy that we must fight. It will be defeated once it will be possible to establish the communist economic regime, but it’s necessary to attack it even today.

The whole bourgeois environment leads thus to individualism. Our socialist, anti-bourgeois struggle, our revolutionary preparation should be directed towards *laying the foundations for the new environment*.

Here is how we envision the whole programme of the youth movement. To withdraw the cultivation of man from the exclusive influence of current society, to live all together, we youths, whether workers or not, breathing a different and better atmosphere, to cut the bridges that unite us with non-socialist environments, to sever the ties that allow the poison of selfishness and competition to infiltrate our blood, *to sabotage*, in one word, this infamous society, by creating revolutionary oases destined to one day invade it all, to dig mines destined to shake it up from its bases…

But the article is already too long to expound now on the “concrete” part. We will talk about it another time.

*From* L'Avanguardia*, 1 June 1913. Signed:* Amadeo Bordiga*.*
